# 2026-11-05 — The route map: split pins, a steady zoom, and the real start

## Stops at one address share a split pin

**The report:** on the route map, stops at one house sat on top of each other. In the
field screenshot, 1 was hidden under 2, 3 couldn't be seen, and 6 was half under 7. The
crew couldn't tell how many stops were at a spot, and a tap only reached the top one.

**What shipped:** stops on the same pin, or at the same street address within 50 m, now
share one circle split into a section per stop, each showing its route number. Five or
more show a count (`×8`). Tapping any pin opens a list with the work order, unit and ETA,
and ▲▼ reorder the units at that pin. A move swaps two stops and nothing else, so a pin
holding 3 and 9 trades them and 4 to 8 stay put. It saves the same way as dragging a card.
The route line still runs through each stop's own coordinates.

**The trap:** the first version bound a popup to each marker. Markers are rebuilt on every
redraw, so a close and a reopen could overlap and leave two lists on screen. The test
screenshots hid it, because the delay they add was enough to dodge the race. It is one
shared popup on the map now, and a redraw only swaps its content.

## The route map keeps its zoom when you reorder

**The report:** every reorder zoomed the map back out, and a far-away morning meet made
that worse. The zoom-out on opening the map was fine.

**What shipped:** the map now fits only when it opens or when the day changes.

**The trap:** skipping the fit on the reorder path wouldn't have worked. A reorder reaches
`refresh()` twice, the second time through `planAdvance` → `renderWorklist`, so the fit
is opt-in instead. Overlapping redraws could also leave a second set of pins, so an older
redraw now stops if a newer one has started.

## Optimizing from where you are shows that spot as the start

**The report:** optimizing from the current location still showed the morning meet.

**The cause:** the route was solved from GPS, but the saved drive-out line, which is where
the map reads its start pin from, was always built from the crew start.

**What shipped:** Day 1 now starts at the GPS point until the next stop is logged. Later
days keep the morning meet, because that's where those mornings begin.

**The trap:** the obvious fix was to build the saved line from GPS. That line syncs to the
`Worklist` tab, and the `worklist` read isn't admin-gated, so it would have published
where the installer stood, and broken the guide's promise that the location isn't
uploaded. The point stays on the phone in `wlRouteStartFix`. Other devices and the planner
still show the morning meet.

## Day 1 is picked first: the stops that finish soonest from where you start

**The report:** Day 1 wandered through side streets while Day 2 was one tidy street. The
🔒 work-list lock was off, so the cause was the split itself.

**The cause:** Optimize solved one long path through every stop — furthest from Home
first, with the morning meet left out of the ordering ("ETA-only") — then cut it every
`target` stops into days and re-ordered inside each. Which stops shared a day was an
accident of where the cuts fell, and nothing favoured the day being driven. Two more
things fell out of the same shape: optimizing from the morning meet put the *far* stops on
Day 1 and saved the tidy near-Home street for last, and commute pull had no real 0 — the
master path headed to Home at full strength whatever the dial said.

**Measured first, on the real list** (53 pinned stops, target 24, real road matrix from the
Simcoe district pack, 12 min a stop / 6 for a second meter at the same address / 4.5 min
a km). From the GPS spot the crew optimized from: Day 1 finished 17 min earlier on 0.56 km
instead of 1.80, but ~12 of those minutes were the two same-address pairs landing
back-to-back; Day 2 and Day 3 absorbed the difference and the total over three days was
unchanged. From the morning meet it was the real fix: Day 1 went from the five far stops
plus a trek (8.83 km, done ~14:00) to the near cluster (0.66 km, done ~13:09), 52 minutes
earlier. A swap-improvement search on top of the nearest-neighbour walk changed Day 1 in
no case, so it was not shipped. **Plainly: this does not shorten the week. It makes today
the best day instead of a random one.**

**What shipped:** `solveVariant` builds the days one at a time, today first. Each day takes
the target's worth of stops that finish soonest from where that day starts — a
nearest-neighbour walk out of the start, so a cluster wins — and is ordered from that
start with its end bent toward Home by commute pull. Day 1 leaves the run's start (the
meet, or the GPS fix); **every later day leaves the morning meet**, so a GPS run now carries
the meet as one extra matrix node (`measure.meetIndex`) and the scheduler asks
`fromStart(id, day)` so tomorrow's first ETA is timed from the meet too. Commute pull 0 now
really ignores Home (a pull-0 route is identical to a no-Home route); 100 prefers stops on
the way home and ends pinned to it. A slot-1 lock still starts Day 1 (`solveWithLead` is
folded in as Day 1's pin). The desktop planner shares the code.

**The trap:** the meet node is *appended* after Home, never inserted, so every order keeps
its matrix index and `homeIndex` stays put — three readers (`solveVariant`,
`travelLookup`, `homeLegMetersFor`) must agree on where day 2 starts or the day is modelled
twice. And the gain is honest only against the time model: the 17-minute GPS-start
figure is mostly the dwell model's extra-meter credit, not driving.

## A locked Optimize plans what's left of the day

**The report:** the meters/day target is a day's worth of installs, set in the morning.
A re-plan mid-day — a break, or "No, from where I am" — handed back a fresh full target,
so after 6 installs a locked Optimize to 24 planned 24 more instead of the 18 left.

**What the owner decided:** 🔓 unlocked, Optimize plans a full target on Day 1, exactly as
before, and never looks at installs. 🔒 locked, Optimize plans Day 1 = target − installs
logged today (list orders and walk-ups alike; UTIs don't count), chosen fresh from every
pending order under the Day-1-first rules from where the crew starts; days 2+ fill at the
full target; the new Day 1 replaces the frozen set. Installs at or past the target leave
Day 1 empty and everything on Day 2+ — unlocking is how the crew asks for more. The number
is whatever the target box says at the press (unlock → type 20 → lock → Optimize gives
20 − 6 = 14). The desktop planner does the same, reading that installer's installs from
the sheet (`?action=day`).

**What shipped:** `route-today.js installCount` (printable stops that are not UTIs),
`remainingDay1` (floored at 0) and `strictDay1Ids`. `optimizeRoute` takes `day1Count`
and `solveVariant` picks that many for Day 1, with `perDay` for days 2+ and a slot-1
lead keeping its place even on a day of nothing; the phone's `optimizeRouteHandler` and
the planner's `optimize` hand the **same** number to `scheduleRouteConstraints`
(`opts.day1Count`) and write the result back to `wlTodayAnchor`. The finished toast says
*🔒 Day 1: 18 left of 24 (6 installed)*.

**The trap:** this is a deliberate carve-out from "nothing may re-size a locked day from
meters installed", and it is an exception *because it is a press*. Nothing passive
changed — a logged stop, the Drive screen's 3-minute refresh, a Download and the per-log
`applyTodayAnchor` still hold the day as it stands, and `remainingDay1` has exactly one
caller per page. Three details earn their test each: the count is refreshed before it is
read (a second phone the crew only drives by otherwise believes nothing was installed);
0 is a real value at every hop, never a falsy "unset"; and the anchor is rewritten from
the *strict* day-1 tags, because `taggedDay1Ids` takes the lowest day present and a spent
Day 1 would have frozen tomorrow as today.
