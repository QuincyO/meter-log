# 2026-11-18 — A UTI copies a note for the supervisor; emergency electrical repairs get their own reason; every address gets its own pin; the route map shows every order at once

## Logging a UTI copies the work order number, then a note

**The problem:** after a UTI the installer messages their supervisor the work order and
the reason. The app only copied the work order number, and only in plan mode, so the
message was typed by hand at the door.

**What shipped:** logging a UTI now makes two copies, one after the other.

1. The work order number, as before.
2. About half a second later, the note:

```
Work order: 1022668
No Access - gate locked, dog loose
```

The second line is the reason, then the stop's Note when there is one. The note is what
is on the clipboard afterwards, and the number is one entry back in the keyboard's
clipboard history (Gboard keeps it for an hour), ready for the meter-install app. It
happens on every UTI, plan mode or not. An install is unchanged: the number alone, and
only in plan mode.

**The traps:**

- **"Work order" is two words on purpose.** The first request was `Workorder: #`. One
  word and a colon is a valid URI scheme, and that is what an iPhone pasted to the office
  as `%0A` and `%3A` on [2026-08-14](2026-08-14.md). `new URL('Workorder: 1022668…')`
  parses; `new URL('Work order: 1022668…')` throws, because a scheme cannot contain a
  space. The test asserts both.
- **The two copies are apart on purpose** (`UTI_NOTE_COPY_GAP_MS`, 600 ms). Fired back to
  back they can reach the keyboard as one clipboard change, and the number never enters
  the history. This is the one value that was not measured on a phone. If the number is
  missing from Gboard's history, raise it. Keep it short: a page that has lost focus to
  the next app is refused the write, so switching apps before the "UTI note copied"
  message leaves the number on the clipboard and no note.
- **The number is the synchronous write.** If the second write is refused, the phone is
  left exactly where it was before this change.
- **`utiNoteText` is reached through `import * as utiLib` and a `typeof` check.** A phone
  can run the new `capture.js` against an old `utiReasons.js` until somebody presses
  ⟳ Force update, and a missing named export there is a link error that takes Log stop
  with it.

## Emergency Electrical Repair is a new UTI reason, and the only EER

**The problem:** the end-of-day tally counted every **Electrical Repair** UTI on its
`EER` line. A normal electrical repair is just a UTI. Only an emergency one is an EER.

**What shipped:** **Emergency Electrical Repair** is in the reason dropdown, right under
Electrical Repair, on the capture form, the Today card and `edit.html`. `EER_UTI_REASON`
now names it, so the tally's `EER` line counts those and nothing else. It still counts on
the `UTI` line too, as before, so `UTI` keeps matching the PDF's UTI total.

**The trap:** a stop logged as Electrical Repair before this change no longer counts as
EER. If one was really an emergency, change its reason on the Today card before
generating the log.

## Nine addresses on one pin: a road is not a town

**The problem:** an Optimize tap asked "select which town" for Falconbridge Dr and
Inadale Dr. Southwest Middlesex was picked, and the route map showed two pins, ×9 and
×6, for fifteen different addresses.

**The cause**, reproduced against the published district: the offline address index
placed a house number once per *locality*, and the locality tag in the map is the
municipality. Falconbridge Dr is one road, numbered 3701–6580 in Southwest Middlesex
and 6581–9152 in Strathroy-Caradoc. So 8583 came back twice: correctly, between 8561
and 8978, and again in Southwest Middlesex, clamped to that town's last point because
its numbers stop at 6580. Two towns, so the tap asked. Either answer looked valid, and
the clamped one put every number above 6580 on the same point, 10 km from the houses.
Inadale Dr is the same road-through-three-townships shape, and its orders (8853–8962)
are also past the last number the map has (8501).

**What shipped** (all in the offline index, `geocodeAddress`):

- **Towns that share a road are one road.** Localities whose neighbouring numbers sit
  within 500 m of each other are joined, so the number is placed once, on the whole road.
- **A town whose numbers don't reach the typed one is not offered** while another's do.
- **A town on the end of the address is read**, with or without a comma. It picks
  between two different roads of the same name. It never moves the pin along a road,
  and a town the map doesn't know (the crew types Glencoe, the map says Southwest
  Middlesex) is ignored instead of failing the lookup.
- **An estimated pin is moved onto the road of that name**, and a number past the last
  one known carries on along that road at the rate the numbers were climbing.
- **A real tie goes to the nearest town** when it is at least twice as close to the crew
  as the next one. A closer call still asks, nearest first. This is the owner's rule,
  "the town closest to me is likely the right address", and it replaces "never
  auto-picked" on the tap only.
- **Different addresses on one exact pin are looked up again on every Optimize.** This
  is what repairs the fifteen orders already pinned: tap Optimize once after updating.

**The traps:**

- **Don't join roads through the untagged bucket.** 40% of the district's address
  points have no town, and they all share one bucket per street name. One bridge
  through it merges every street of that name in the district.
- **The 5 km bracket rule is not a distance cap.** A bracket that wide only counts when
  a road of that name runs under the estimate. A plain cap broke the case interpolation
  exists for, two address points on a 12 km concession, and its own test caught it.
- **The nearest-town rule is wrong sometimes, by design.** When the same number exists
  on the same street name in the next city and that one is nearer the crew, it is
  pinned without asking. Measured below. Typing the town on the address overrides it.
- **`repinShared` only ever asks the district index.** A tap stays offline, and an
  order that was routable is never parked by a second opinion.

## The route map has an All tab

**The problem:** the route map showed one day at a time. The stacked pins above were
on Day 5, so nothing showed them until that tab was opened. The owner asked for a way
to see every order on the map at once, to know where the work is and to catch a bad
pin from the start.

**What shipped:** when the route has more than one day, the first button above the map
is **All · N**.

- Every pending order with a pin is on the map. A pin shows its **day number** in that
  day's colour. There is no route line and nothing can be reordered here.
- Several orders on one spot show the count, like **×4**. Tapping a pin lists its
  orders with their day and stop number, and says so when they are different addresses.
- Under the map there is one row per day, in the pin colour. Tap a row to open that day.
- **The yellow note now counts orders sharing a pin with a different address**, on
  every tab. It is the same test Optimize uses to look those orders up again
  (`sharedPinIds`).

**The traps:**

- **No new markup and no new CSS.** A phone can run new JS against an older
  `index.html` and stylesheet for as long as nobody presses ⟳ Force update, so the tab,
  the day rows and the pin colours all use classes that already exist.
- **`sharedPinIds` comes through `import * as routeLib`.** As a named import it would be
  a link error against an older cached `route.js`, and the whole route map would go.
- **The tab is hidden with one group.** That group's own tab already shows every order.

## The spine measures the usual first log and the delay per stop

**The problem:** every ETA assumes the crew leaves at 08:15, whatever their real habit.
Nothing measured when an installer actually starts or how much time a stop costs beyond
driving and breaks.

**What shipped:** `installerDayShape` in `Code.gs` reads an installer's `Stops` and
returns the usual first log (median, minutes after midnight) and the delay per stop.
They are stored on `InstallerMetrics` as `usualFirstLogMin` and `delayPerStopMin`, in
the combined, `boat*` and `land*` groups, appended as the last six columns, and
`installerMetricsRead` projects both for `workType=boat|land`. A day counts at 5 or more
logs, the last 30 such days are read, and under 10 days both are blank. This change only
stores them; the phone, the planner and the route map read them from the changes below.

**The trap:** the delay is net of 60 minutes (`DAY_BREAK_MIN`) for the three planned
breaks, floored at zero, so a day with less overhead than the breaks reads 0, never a
negative. And every installer reads blank until `backfillInstallerMetrics()` is run once
from the Apps Script editor after `Code.gs` deploys.

[AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md), [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md).

## The scheduler can take an arrival clock, and an early appointment pulls it earlier

**The problem:** `scheduleRouteConstraints` only knew a departure clock and added the
drive out. An installer's usual first log is a measured arrival, so using it would count
the morning drive twice, and an appointment earlier than that clock would arrive late.

**What shipped:** a new opt-in `opts.firstStopArrival`. When true, `firstStopTime` is the
time the crew arrives at a day's first stop and no drive out is added. An arrival-anchored
day whose earliest appointment is before that clock starts 20 minutes before the
appointment, for that day only. An appointment at or after the clock leaves it alone. No caller passed the flag in this change; the phone does from the usual-start change below.

**The trap:** `day1FirstStopTime` is still a departure (a re-plan from where the crew
stands), so Day 1 on it is not arrival-anchored. The pull-earlier clock is ETAs only and
never touches day sizing.

Files: [route-constraints.js](../js/route-constraints.js),
[tests](../tests/route-constraints.test.mjs), [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

## The day's end time and the flat-out number, as pure functions

**The problem:** the route map is about to show when each day should end and how many stops
a day could hold before the cut-off. The arithmetic had nowhere to live.

**What shipped:** three pure functions in `js/route-constraints.js` and three constants in
`js/config.js` (`ROUTE_USUAL_START` 09:30, `ROUTE_BREAK_MIN` 60, a daily total, and
`ROUTE_FLAT_OUT_END` 15:30). `usualStartClock` turns the measured first log into a
first-arrival clock, or the fallback when the measurement is blank or outside 05:00 to 14:00.
`dayEndEstimate` adds a day's work, the installer's delay per stop and the break minutes the caller
supplies. `breakMinutesLeft` works out how much of the 60 is left on a day already begun.
`flatOutPerDay` finds the most stops that fit between a start and the cut-off. This change
added no caller, so nothing changed in the field with it; the phone and the route map call
them from the changes below.

**The trap:** breaks are 60 minutes a day taken whenever and usually not logged, not three set
times. A first version walked 10:00, 12:00 and 14:00 across the day; the owner corrected it, so the
clock walk is gone and the minutes are one number the caller passes. Today's first log replaces
only the reported start.
None of this may size a day: a test checks that `scheduleRouteConstraints`, `simulateDay`,
`placeAppointments`, `route.js` and `route-today.js` never mention these names, or the new
break names.

## The phone's ETAs start from the usual start

**The problem:** every ETA on the phone assumed the crew left the muster point at 08:15 and
added the drive out. Real crews reach their first stop between 09:30 and 10:00, so the morning
ETAs were an hour or more early, and every one after them carried the same error.

**What shipped:** each day's first ETA on the phone is now the installer's usual first-stop
ARRIVAL: the usual first log the spine measures, less one on-site time, or 09:30 with no
measurement (an older spine that does not send the field gets 09:30 too). The worklist reads it
with the pace from `installerMetrics` and keeps it on the phone, with the delay per stop beside
it. The scheduler gets it as an arrival clock, so no drive out is added, and a day with an
appointment before it starts 20 minutes before that appointment, with that appointment as its first stop. Today's route runs from the
time it is now once a stop has been logged today, however early, or once the usual start has
passed (the final fixes below make that Day 1's own clock, which an early appointment pulls
earlier). The day divider's length is measured from that day's own first ETA, and its tooltip no
longer claims a drive out except on a Day 1 running from where the crew stands. This change
left the desktop planner on 08:15; it moved to the same start later the same day (below).

**The trap:** `planShape` now calls `usualStart`, which calls `dwellShape`, which used to call
`planShape` for the pace. That loop would overflow the stack on the first render, so both read
the pace box through `paceVal`. And `firstStopArrival` is not a sheet field: `savePlanLocal`
strips it, so no plan push carries it.

Checked in headless Edge with the spine stubbed: a measured 09:57 first log with a 12-minute job
puts Day 2's first ETA at 09:45; no fields gives 09:30; an 08:30 appointment on Day 2 is that
day's first stop at 08:10 with no late badge; and a stop logged at 08:50 with the clock at 09:00 runs Day 1
from 09:00.

## A pulled day puts its appointment first

**The problem:** an appointment earlier than the usual start pulls its day to 20 minutes before
it, so the crew can be there when the window opens. But the slot search breaks a zero-wait tie
by taking the later slot, and with a short on-site time slot 2 was on time too. So an ordinary
stop took the 08:10 start and the 08:30 appointment came second at 08:23 (seen in the headless
Edge check of the usual-start change).

**What shipped:** on a pulled day the appointment that pulled it (the earliest that day) is fixed
to slot 1, decided once in `dayConstraints`, which both the normal and the reflow path now use,
so the search and the final walk see the same day. A second early appointment that day is still
placed by the search. A day that is not pulled, and every run without `firstStopArrival`, is
unchanged.

**The trap:** an order locked to slot 1 wins, and the search runs as before. On the reflow path
the slot-1 `keepAfter` lead is now only a real lock: a pulled appointment in slot 1 was never
grown from by the solver, so the route carries on from it to the stops near it. Slots only: no
day count changes.

## The All tab shows each day's end time and the flat-out number

**The problem:** a day row on the route map's All tab showed only its first-to-last arrival
window, such as 09:45–14:05. That is when the crew reaches the last stop, not when the day is
over: it leaves out the last job, the breaks and the delays every stop picks up. The owner also
wanted to see how many meters a day could really hold before 15:30.

**What shipped:** each day row now reads like `09:45 → ends about 15:55 · 60 min breaks · 50 min
delays · tap to open`. The end is the route's own work, plus 60 minutes of breaks, plus the
installer's measured delay per stop for every stop. Day 1 of a plan for today gets only the
breaks still to come (the smaller of what the logged breaks leave and the share for the stops
left), and once a stop is logged today it starts `started 08:50`. Under the rows, a muted line
says `Flat out: about 22 a day by 15:30 — all 113 in 6 days. Planned at 25 a day.`, worked out
from the usual start, the full 60 minutes of breaks, the delays, and the work per stop the
optimized days show. Every installer sees their own figures.

**The trap:** this is display only. The meters/day target alone sizes a day; nothing here
reaches it, `counts[]` or the Optimize solve. The view reaches the scheduler through a namespace
with a `typeof` check and gets the constants inside the plan, so a phone with an older cached
`route-constraints.js` keeps the old window and shows no flat-out line. Checked against the
live figures, the flat-out number is 18 to 21 a day, at or a little below what the crew already
averages (20 to 22) and below the target of 25. It is held down by the start time (09:18 to
10:09), not by the work: a different cut-off, or leaving the delays out of this one figure, is
the owner's call.

Checked in headless Edge with the spine stubbed: with metrics each day reads `09:45 → ends about
12:00 · 60 min breaks · 14 min delays` and flat out is 19 a day; without metrics `09:30 → ends
about 11:31 · 60 min breaks` and 24 a day; a list with no ETAs keeps the plain rows and no line;
and Day 1 re-anchored at 17:14 after 18 stops and a 30-minute lunch reads `started 08:50 → ends
about 18:42 · 13 min breaks · 14 min delays`.

## The desktop planner uses the same start

**The problem:** the phone's ETAs moved onto each installer's usual first-stop arrival, but the
office's planner still timed every day from an 08:15 departure plus the drive out. A list the
office planned and uploaded showed one set of times on the planner and a later set on the phone,
for the same orders.

**What shipped:** the planner now starts each day's first stop at the picked installer's usual
arrival, the same clock the phone uses: their usual first log, from the `installerMetrics` read
the planner already makes for the pace, less one on-site time. Without a measurement, or while
the read is still out or has failed, it plans on 09:30. Switching installers clears the figure,
and a reply that arrives after the office has picked someone else is ignored, so one installer's
start never lands on another's list.

**The trap:** `dwellShape` used to call `planShape` for the pace, and `planShape` now reaches
`dwellShape` through `usualStart`, so both read the pace box through a new `paceVal` instead.
`firstStopArrival` is not a sheet column: the upload drops it and keeps `firstStopTime`.
`ROUTE_DEPART_TIME` stays, because `optimizeRoute`'s `departMin` still names it, but nothing
schedules on it any more; `config.js` says so.

installer with a usual first log of 547 and an on-site time of 14 gets a Day 2 that starts at

## Final fixes to the usual start and the day end

**The problem:** a review of the whole usual-start work found five things wrong in the field and
some text that no longer told the truth.
- A Day 1 pulled early by an appointment did not go live when its own clock passed. At 09:00, with
  nothing logged, a usual start of 10:10 and an 08:30 appointment, the phone kept the pulled plan:
  the appointment "on time" at 08:10 and a morning of ETAs already in the past.
- The flat-out line showed on a list nobody had optimized. Opening the worklist times every
  pending order, so the figure came from the order the orders were entered in.
- The list was timed when the worklist opened, before the metrics read answered, so on the day
  this ships every phone showed 09:30-based ETAs until the next open or logged stop.
- A phone pairing the new route map with a cached older scheduler would drop the 60 break minutes
  without a word, because the first versions of the end-time functions ignored them.
- The tuning screen still said the leave-start time was 08:15 for everyone.

**What shipped:** Day 1 goes live once a stop is logged today or once now is past Day 1's own
clock: the usual start, or 20 minutes before the earliest pending appointment dated today when
that is earlier, the same pull the scheduler gives the day. The flat-out line now needs at least
one pending order with a saved route order from an Optimize, on the phone or the planner; the day
rows keep their end times either way. When the metrics read changes the stored usual first log,
the list is re-timed once through the same path a logged stop takes, so Day 2's first ETA moves to
the installer's own start without reopening anything. The route map's end time and flat-out line
also need the scheduler that understands breaks, and say nothing with an older one. The tuning
screen now says ETAs start from the installer's own usual first-stop time, taken from their last
30 land working days, or 09:30 until there are 10 such days. Stale 08:15 comments, a false
"nothing reads these yet" in `ARCHITECTURE.md`, and the "not yet" lines in this page and the
index row are corrected.

**The trap:** Day 1's own clock is worked out from the appointments on the list, never from a
stored first ETA: once the day is live that ETA moves with the clock, and the answer would flip
back and forth. The appointments come from the last read of the list (`allSorted` keeps them),
because `day1DepartTime` is synchronous and runs inside every render. An appointment at or after
the usual start does not pull the day, as in the scheduler. The re-time on a new usual start is
skipped while an Optimize is running, and it never re-solves the route or moves an order to
another day.

Checked in headless Edge with the spine stubbed. At 09:00 with nothing logged, a usual start of
10:10 and an 08:30 appointment today, Day 1 runs from 09:00 and the appointment reads `ETA 09:00`
