# 2026-07-27 — The route map keeps its road lines all day, the hold press means something again, a raised target can finally unfreeze Day 1, time at a stop stops being a guess, the phone can finally plan tomorrow, the day divider stops guessing how long a day is, the measured on-site time stops being stored as a 1900 date, and an appointment stops being scheduled against a day that does not exist

## A Day 1 frozen at 6 meters/day stayed six orders forever

`js/route-today.js`, `js/worklist.js`, `tests/route-today.test.mjs`,
`AGENTS.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `USER-GUIDE.md`

Reported as "my target is 24 but the first day only has 6 orders" — from a route built
earlier under a target of 6. Re-optimizing didn't fix it, and it survived into the next
day.

**The today anchor had no idea what target sized it.** `needsCommit` re-commits the
frozen set on exactly three conditions: no anchor, a stale date, or every committed order
finished. A changed target is not one of them, so the six ids stayed and `day1Count`
capped Day 1 at `min(capacity 24, 6 ids) = 6`.

The overnight half is the part that made it feel unfixable. On the first view of a new
day the anchor *is* re-committed — but `freshAnchorIds` prefers the `day` tags already on
the orders, and those were stamped by the target-6 solve. So it re-froze at 6 **before**
Optimize was ever pressed, and the identity rule then refused to move. That tag
preference is right in general (it honours the optimizer's time-capacity shrink, the
office's chunking, an appointment day); it is only poison when the tags were made under a
different target.

The anchor now records the target it was frozen under, and `needsCommit` gains a fourth
reason: a **re-plan whose target moved**. Four decisions worth keeping:

- **Only an explicit Optimize passes `replan`.** Typing in the Meters/day box is not a
  re-plan; the press is — and it is also the moment the days have just been re-solved at
  the new target, so the fresh commit reads tags that match it. `planAdvance`, Download,
  first view, `markWorklistDone` and `resetWorklistOrder` all pass nothing and keep today
  frozen exactly as before.
- **An Optimize at an *unchanged* target still never unfreezes today.** That is the
  original bug the anchor exists to prevent — finish a few, re-optimize, and tomorrow's
  work walks up into today. There is a test whose only job is to fail if someone
  "simplifies" the target comparison away.
- **A mid-day re-plan is bounded by the room actually left** (`freshAnchorIds`'
  `opts.max` = `dayCapacity`). Raise the target at noon with 6 already in and today grows
  to 18 more, not a fresh 24 hauled up out of tomorrow. A day with no installs *and* no
  UTIs, or one already closed out, is a day nobody has driven — those reshuffle freely.
- **A legacy anchor reads as changed on purpose.** It is the one-shot that unsticks the
  reported day: without it the fix would only help routes frozen *after* it shipped, and
  the installer's jammed six-order day would need the target toggled off and back to
  clear itself.

Two coercion traps handled rather than discovered later: `opts.max` must treat
`null`/absent as *unbounded*, because `Number(null)` is 0 and would silently freeze an
**empty** day on the one path that means "no limit"; and a live day at `capacity === 0`
skips the re-plan entirely, since an empty committed set makes `needsCommit` fire again
on every subsequent call. A met target is a full day, not an exhausted one.

Phone-local throughout — the anchor is never synced, so no spine action, no sheet column,
nothing added to `SHELL`. Seven new tests (370 → 377).

Driven in headless Edge as well as asserted, off a seeded 30-order worklist with every
spine and geocoder request stubbed (pinned orders skip geocoding, so a straight-line tap
makes no matrix call at all):

```
cold, target 6            → Day 1 = 6   anchor {n:6,  target:6}
Optimize at 6             → Day 1 = 6   unchanged
raise to 24, Optimize     → Day 1 = 16  anchor {n:16, target:24}   ← re-planned
Optimize again at 24      → Day 1 = 16  unchanged                  ← freeze holds
mid-day (5 installed),
  raise 6 → 24, Optimize  → Day 1 = 16  ≤ capacity 19, not a fresh 24
```

Day 1 lands on 16 rather than 24 because `timeCapacity` sizes the day to finish by ~14:00
— the optimizer's shrink, which is exactly what preferring the freshly-solved `day` tags
is there to honour.

One harness trap worth recording for the next person driving this page: seeding
`localStorage` while `initWorklist` is still in flight races the `#wlTarget` restore, and
`targetVal()` falls back to its **24** default on a blank input. That produced an anchor
committed at 24 with the store saying 6 — a phantom that looks exactly like the bug being
fixed. Let the page settle before seeding.

## The Optimize press picks the ladder: tap measures on the phone, hold asks the network

`js/worklist.js`, `js/route.js`, `tests/route.test.mjs`,
`tests/press-hold.test.mjs`, `tests/worklist-start-location.test.mjs`,
`AGENTS.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `USER-GUIDE.md`

When offline packs landed, the on-device road graph was put at the top of the matrix
ladder for **both** presses, and the entry celebrating that said the quiet part: "the
two-second hold no longer means spend money — a plain tap is road-accurate wherever a
pack covers the run, and the gesture only changes anything outside it."

That was the right call for cost and the wrong one for control. Inside a downloaded
district the hold became a no-op, and there was no longer any way to get a second
opinion — which matters precisely because **the pack carries no turn restrictions**. It
knows the geometry turns left; it cannot know the left turn is illegal. That is the same
limitation the desktop planner keeps a real OSRM for, and on the phone it had quietly
become unanswerable.

So the press now picks the ladder rather than ranking within one:

| press | matrix order |
|---|---|
| **tap** | district pack → straight-line |
| **hold** (2s) | *pack skipped* → Google Routes → OpenRouteService → straight-line |

The mechanism was already in `route.js` — `opts.noLocalGraph` guarded the local-graph
rung and no caller had ever set it. The change is the worklist handler taking a
`useNetwork` flag from the gesture and passing `{straightLine: !useNetwork, noLocalGraph:
useNetwork}`. The desktop planner is untouched: `opts.osrmUrl` already excluded the graph
and its ladder is still OSRM → ORS → straight-line, never Google.

Two consequences that had to be handled rather than discovered later:

- **A hold with no signal is now refused.** It skips the pack by definition, so offline it
  has nothing at all to measure with — the old gate let it through on the strength of a
  pack it no longer uses, which would have produced a silent straight-line route dressed
  up as a road one. The toast points back at the tap that would have worked.
- **Both presses now ask for `compareVariants`.** A pack-routed tap *is* a road run, so
  it has a road route worth comparing — but it was passing `compareVariants: !straightLine`
  and therefore saving a road route with no straight-line counterpart, leaving the
  road/straight toggle greyed out with nothing to switch to. Asking on both presses costs
  one extra local solve and can never trigger a matrix call: `route.js` only reads the flag
  inside `if(onRoad)`.

The live-GPS-start crow-flies rewrite lands on the right side of the split for free. It
exists to keep a one-run fix out of a matrix somebody pays for, and it is skipped when
`usedLocalGraph` — which is now false on exactly the run that costs money.

Four new tests (366 → 370). The two behavioural ones read the routing **provider events**
rather than stubbing modules: `roadgraph` is emitted before the pack is even loaded, so
its absence proves the rung was skipped rather than tried and failed.

Driven in headless Edge as well as asserted, because the press is the part a test can only
read as source text. All four states of the pre-run message, off a seeded three-order
worklist:

```
pack,   tap  → Optimizing 3 orders — offline road map on this phone.
pack,   hold → Optimizing 3 orders — Google road distances (then OpenRouteService, then straight-line).
no pack, tap → Optimizing 3 orders — straight-line algorithm.
offline, hold, pack held → Offline — holding needs signal; tap Optimize to use the offline map
offline, hold, no pack   → Offline — road distances need signal; tap Optimize for straight-line
offline, tap,  pack held → runs (the tap never needed signal)
```

`node --test` 370/370.

## Road paths measured at draw time, not trusted from the last Optimize

`js/route.js`, `js/worklist-route-view.js`, `tests/worklist-route-view.test.mjs`,
`AGENTS.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`

Reported as "the optimized route should follow the roads" — which read like a missing
feature, and wasn't. Every piece was already there: the phone measures real road paths
off the district pack during Optimize (`localRoadGeometry`), stores them in
`legGeometryRoad`, and `worklist-route-view.js` decodes them onto the map. On a phone
with a district downloaded, the correct polylines were being written and then never
drawn.

**The geometry was right; the gate was shutting on it.** A saved path is keyed to the
sequence it was fetched for, so both maps check `variantMatchesLive` — does the live
`order` still match the variant's `orderRoad`? — before drawing any of it. That check is
correct in intent: after a reorder, leg *n* describes a drive between two stops that are
no longer adjacent, and drawing it traces the previous route's roads.

What made it fire constantly is that the phone reorders the list on its own.
`applyTodayAnchor` hoists the frozen today-set to the front of the pending list and
rewrites `order` — and it never touches `orderRoad`, because the variant is supposed to
record what the optimizer decided. So the two sequences diverge by design, and the gate
is all-or-nothing: one moved order drops the road lines for the entire day. Worse, the
anchor runs after **every logged stop** via `planAdvance`, not only on a re-optimize, so
a route that started the morning drawn on roads reverted to straight lines as the crew
worked. Reproduced against the real modules: geometry stored for 5 of 5 legs,
`variantMatchesLive` false, nothing drawn.

**The fix removes the question rather than answering it.** `offlineRoutePaths(legs)` in
`js/route.js` takes the legs the map is *about to draw* and measures those against the
pack — one shared decode, the run's own coordinates picking the district exactly as the
matrix does. Paths derived from what is on screen cannot be stale, so no gate applies to
them. Precedence is **measured → saved → straight**.

The saved tier keeps its `variantMatchesLive` gate, and that is deliberate rather than
leftover: on a phone with no district of its own, that polyline is the desktop planner's
OSRM path arriving over the sync, and it can genuinely be stale. This is also why the fix
is phone-side only — the planner has no anchor step, so its geometry stays keyed to the
sequence it was fetched for, and it still needs a live OSRM for turn restrictions the
pack doesn't carry.

Three details worth keeping:

- **`legKey()` is exported for both sides.** The caller assembling the leg list and
  `buildRouteMapModel` looking paths up have to agree on the key; a silent mismatch
  degrades to straight lines rather than erroring, which is exactly the failure this
  change exists to stop being invisible.
- **The drive-out's saved polyline is read even when the gate is shut.** Its first point
  is the only record of where the crew start is — the crew start is not an order and has
  no row of its own — so the start pin survives a reorder even though only the leg's
  shape is re-measured. A first stop carrying no saved drive-out draws no start pin at
  all, which is the intended trade: no pin beats a wrong one.
- **A leg the pack can't carry is absent from the result, not an error.** Off-network
  ends, nothing connecting them, no pack installed — all land on the same fallback the
  map already had.

No schema change, no spine change, nothing added to `SHELL`. Six new tests
(360 → 366), including the reproduction: a leg drawn from a measured path while the
saved geometry is gated off.

## On-site time is measured now, and it varies per stop

`js/route-dwell.js` (new), `tests/route-dwell.test.mjs` (new),
`tests/route-dwell-parity.test.mjs` (new), `tests/dwell-measure.test.mjs` (new),
`tests/installer-metrics-dwell-schema.test.mjs` (new), `Code.gs`,
`js/route-constraints.js`, `js/route.js`, `js/worklist.js`, `js/worklist-tuning.js`,
`js/worklist-route-view.js`, `js/pages/planner.js`, `index.html`,
`tests/route-constraints.test.mjs`, `AGENTS.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `USER-GUIDE.md`

Reported as "the route shows the ETA from one stop to another, but doesn't factor in
how long the installer is at each order". Half right, and the half that was wrong is
the more useful half: it *was* factored in, as

```js
export const NOMINAL_TRAVEL_MIN = 10;
onSiteMinutes(pace) = max(8, pace − 10)
```

— one number for every stop on every route, where the `10` stood in for "the drive
that a log-to-log pace already contains, which the scheduler is about to add back for
real". Nobody had ever checked it against anything.

**The sample that fixes it was already being collected.** For two consecutive logged
stops, `gap = drive to it + work at it + interruptions`, and the interruptions are
exactly the gap-tagged `Downtime` rows the crew allocates in the end-of-day travel
review. Subtract those and regress the remainder against the distance moved: the
**intercept** is the on-site work and the **slope** is the installer's real travel
rate — the very thing the hardcoded ten was guessing. No new taps, no new order
fields, nothing to backfill by hand.

Measured against the real export, the guess was close for the boat crew (Quincy 28
measured vs 28 guessed, Phillip 25 vs 27) and badly off for land — Brody **43
measured vs 57 guessed**, a 14-minute error at every stop.

**Three tiers, each falling through to the next.** A repeat meter at an address the
crew is already parked at takes `extraMeterMin` — measured at 11–12 min against a
25–29 min fresh site, and about **11% of all gaps are these**; a site with a track
record scales the base; everything else takes the measured base. With nothing
measured it is `max(8, pace − 10)` exactly as before, which is the state every
installer is in until `backfillInstallerMetrics()` runs.

Notes worth keeping:

- **`opts.dwell` mirrors `opts.travel`, including its trap.** `scheduleRouteConstraints`
  prices per stop only when handed one; a caller that forgets it silently gets the old
  flat number with no error — the same footgun behind the "8:15, no drive-out" bug.
  Every optimize path passes both, and `timeCapacity` takes the same model's average,
  because a day target that disagrees with the ETAs it sizes is worse than both being
  wrong the same way.
- **Trim by residual, not by gap minutes.** Dropping the slowest 10% before fitting
  looks equivalent and is not: the slowest gaps are mostly the longest drives, so it
  shaves the far end off the distance distribution, drags the slope down and pushes the
  intercept — the number actually shipped — *up*. A residual trim removes
  "surprisingly slow for this distance", which is what an unlogged delay looks like.
- **The site key is civic number + street, with the locality tail cut.** 396 of 457
  logged stops carry a full geocoder string ("10 Island 19c, Carling, ON P0G 1G0,
  Canada") while a worklist order carries what the office typed ("10 Island 19c"), so
  keeping the tail meant the two sides could never agree and the tier would have
  silently never fired once. Taking comma-segment zero is *also* wrong: some addresses
  are label-prefixed ("Town of, 14 Maple Heights Dr, …"), which collapsed 34 distinct
  Huntsville houses onto the key `town of` — inventing a busy site out of unrelated
  stops, worse than not matching at all. It takes the first segment starting with a
  civic number. Never keyed on coordinates: a Stops pin is a phone GPS fix and jitters
  metres between visits to one property.
- **The site tier is built and currently inert, deliberately.** The export holds 266
  sites visited once and 22 twice, and those n=2 factors spanned 0.34–4.03 — noise
  wearing a number, and it would have driven real ETAs. The bar is four visits *plus* a
  usable travel rate: without a rate, "work" is just the raw gap and a remote site
  scores slow purely for being remote — the live case on boat routes, where the
  distance/time slope is dominated by docking. Almost nothing qualifies today and the
  tier does nothing, which is the right amount to do with no evidence; it starts
  contributing on its own as repeat visits accumulate, with no code change.
- **GPS sits above the fit.** The hole between one `DriveTracks` leg ending and the
  next starting is literally time parked at a stop — true arrive/depart, the one thing
  a Stops timestamp can never be, since the app records one moment per stop and no
  arrival. Recording is opt-in per phone per day, so most installers land on the fit;
  `onSiteSource` (`gps`/`fit`/`pace`) records which, and the readouts quote it.
- **No new `Worklist` columns.** The per-stop dwell rides in IndexedDB only —
  `wireShape()` is an explicit allow-list, so it never reaches the sheet and the sync is
  untouched.
- **`siteKeyOf` in `Code.gs` is a hand copy** of `siteKey` (Apps Script cannot import a
  module). `tests/route-dwell-parity.test.mjs` evaluates the real Apps Script source and
  holds both to the same output — a drift there fails in the worst way available: the
  spine keys history one way, the phone looks it up another, no error, no wrong number,
  the feature simply never fires.

Also visible now, since the complaint was partly that none of this was: each route card
carries `· 21m on site` beside its ETA, and the tuning screen shows the number with its
provenance plus an override that wins over the measurement (kept in its own key, so
clearing the box hands the measurement back).

39 new tests (375 → 414), including the first coverage the spine-side arithmetic has
ever had — `tests/dwell-measure.test.mjs` evaluates the real `Code.gs` against fixtures
with a known right answer and checks the fit recovers it.

Driven in headless Edge with every spine call intercepted (`Code.gs` isn't deployed
until this pushes, and nothing may touch the production Sheet), off a seeded four-order
worklist whose 2nd and 3rd orders share an address:

```
measured (onSiteMin 21, extraMeterMin 5, site "9 lake street" ×1.8):
  W1  1 Lake St   08:15   21m on site     ← measured base
  W2  9 Lake St   08:39   38m on site     ← 21 × 1.8 site factor
  W3  9 Lake St   09:17    5m on site     ← repeat meter, beats the factor
  W4 20 Lake St   09:26   21m on site

```

**Deploying takes three steps and the middle one is easy to forget:** push (CI deploys
`Code.gs`), run `setupSheets()` once for the appended `InstallerMetrics` columns, then
`backfillInstallerMetrics()` to populate the fit from history. Until that last one runs
every installer reads `onSiteSource: 'pace'` and behaves exactly as before — the
intended safe default.

## Every phone route was dated today, because "nextWeekday" wasn't

`js/route-planday.js` (new), `tests/route-planday.test.mjs` (new), `js/worklist.js`,
`js/pages/planner.js`, `Code.gs`, `index.html`, `css/capture.css`,
`tests/worklist-tuning-sync.test.mjs`, `AGENTS.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `USER-GUIDE.md`

Reported from the field at gone 5pm: "I'm planning tomorrow, I've been given an
appointment so I added the date and time, but the route that gets generated is for
today — and all the work orders are locked for today." The installer hadn't worked
that day at all.

**One line, and a function whose name lied.**

```js
routeStartDate: nextWeekday(localDate()),   // js/worklist.js
```

`nextWeekday` doesn't advance to tomorrow. It skips *weekends* — so on any weekday it
hands back **today**. Day 1 was therefore always today, and everything followed from
that: `scheduleRouteConstraints` places an appointment at
`workdayOffset(routeStartDate, appointmentDate)`, so an appointment for tomorrow was
offset 1 and landed on **Day 2** by construction, while Day 1 held a full target's
worth of orders for a day that was already over. The today anchor froze that set under
`localDate()` and sized it from `installedToday()`, both hardwired the same way.

**The date isn't the problem; having no way to name a different one is.** The calendar
date is load-bearing in exactly one place — appointments and locks. "Tuesday 10am" is a
promise to a customer and cannot be expressed as a day index. Day chunking, the
meters/day target, ETAs and the anchor are all purely relative and only ever needed a
day *number*. So nothing was stripped out; the phone was given a plan day.

`js/route-planday.js` is the pure decision: `weekdayOnOrAfter` (the old clamp, named
honestly this time), `nextWorkday` (the one that actually advances), and
`resolvePlanDay`, answering **override → rolled → today**. It rolls when today is
closed out, or when nothing has been logged and the installer's `finishBy` clock has
passed. The "Planning for" control on the worklist takes any weekday.

Five decisions worth keeping:

- **A day that is under way never rolls, however late it gets.** The crew is still
  driving it; jumping to tomorrow at 5pm would take their remaining stops off the
  screen. Only a closed-out day, or one nobody ever started, moves on.
- **`anchor.date` is the plan day, not the wall clock.** This is the load-bearing half.
  Freeze an evening-built plan under *today's* date and `needsCommit`'s
  `anchor.date !== today` fires at midnight and re-plans it — destroying exactly the
  evening's work the feature exists to allow. Verified by reload rather than assumed.
- **Two dates, two questions.** `dayClosed()`/`dayStarted()` in the roll decision stay
  on `localDate()` ("is the day I'm standing in spent?"); the capacity tally moved to
  `tallyOn(planDay)` ("how much room does the day I'm planning have?"). A day not yet
  reached has no stops, so planning ahead correctly finds a full day's room instead of
  inheriting what today already spent. Reading either for the other is silent and wrong.
- **A stale override is dropped, not carried.** Set Friday for Friday and by Monday it
  must not still be planning Friday. A weekend pick snaps forward, since
  `scheduleRouteConstraints` refuses a weekend start outright.
- **The office still owns the date on the sheet.** The phone's *implicit* pushes — the
  post-log `syncWorklist` and the `savePlan` riding Download — now omit
  `routeStartDate`, or a phone planning tomorrow would silently re-date the planner's
  route on every logged stop. `saveWorklistPlan` writes the column only when the payload
  carries one; `upsertByHeader` already leaves an unlisted header alone, so absent means
  *keep*, not *blank*. Only the explicit ⇪ Upload publishes it.

One hole the change opens, closed in the same commit: `applyTodayAnchor` swallowed a
`scheduleRouteConstraints` throw with a bare `catch { return; }`. That was near
unreachable while the start date was always today; with a pickable date it is one tap
away — choose Thursday with a Wednesday appointment and the day tags just stop moving.
The message is parked in `wlPlanIssue` and painted on the control instead.

`resolvePlanDay` needs the day's tally (async) while `planShape()` is called
synchronously from a dozen places, so the answer is cached and refreshed at the choke
points that already await. Before the first refresh the accessor answers
**conservatively** — as if the day were under way, so it never rolls on incomplete
information. A wrong "today" self-corrects on the next refresh; a wrong "tomorrow"
would move the installer's route behind their back.

The same coercion trap as the last entry's `opts.max`, in a new place: `Number(null)`
is `0`, so a null clock read as "midnight, past a finish-by of midnight" and rolled
every single call. Caught by the test that exists for it.

The planner's own `nextWeekday` copy is gone too — it now imports `weekdayOnOrAfter`,
behaviour unchanged, because its picker is the real answer there and a second copy of
the misleading name is how this happens twice.

17 new tests (420 → 437). Driven in headless Edge at the reported hour — a real
Monday 18:15, eight pinned orders around Bracebridge/Gravenhurst, every spine call
intercepted, **zero POSTs escaped**:

```

                            Day 1 · 2026-07-28 · 6 meters   appointment ETA 09:40, wait 19m  ← was Day 2
evening, 2 already in     → Planning for 2026-07-27 · "today"
                            Day 1 · 2026-07-27 · 4 meters   (target 6 − 2 installed)
before finish-by          → Planning for 2026-07-27 · "today"   ← unchanged behaviour

```

Phone-local apart from the one-line spine guard; no new column, no new action.
`Code.gs` ships with this push.

## One stale lock took down the whole route — twice over

`js/route-planday.js`, `js/worklist.js`, `tests/route-planday.test.mjs`,
`AGENTS.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`

Reported within the hour, as two separate complaints: "this broke the ability to see
the route's path, now it's just straight lines" and "I change my target and press
optimize and the day's target doesn't change — it locks to whatever it was when I
first optimized". One cause.

**`scheduleRouteConstraints` refuses an order dated before the route starts by
throwing**, and that throw costs the entire route rather than the one order. In
`optimizeRouteHandler` it lands *before* the writes for `order`/`day` **and** before
the writes for `legGeometry*`. So a single rejected order produces two symptoms that
look unrelated: the day sizes stay frozen at whatever the last good solve set (so a
raised meters/day target reads as ignored), and the route map has no saved road
geometry to draw and falls back to straight lines.

**What made every phone hit it at once:** `toggleOrderLock` stamps `lockedDate` from
the order's `scheduledDate`. Before the plan day shipped, every `scheduledDate` was
today — so **every existing lock was dated today**, and the moment the plan day rolled
to tomorrow they all went unsatisfiable together. The previous entry's own worked
example contains the seed of it and nobody noticed: locks are derived state keyed to
the plan day, and moving that day is not a read-only act.

Reproduced against the shipped commit before fixing anything — eight orders, one
locked to today, the real district pack, every spine call intercepted:

```
                          legGeometry legs   day counts   optimize
shipped (broken)                0             none        "WO WO102 is dated before the route starts"
  raise target 4 -> 6           0             none        identical — target ignored
fixed                           5             4 / 4       "Route optimized ✓ · 10 km · 2 days of 4"
  raise target 4 -> 6           4             6 / 2       "… 13 km · 2 days of 6"
```

Two guards, both needed:

- **`resolvePlanDay` clamps to `soonestAppointment`** — the earliest pending
  appointment on or after today. The roll never jumps over a live commitment, which is
  the case that would otherwise break a route the installer had every reason to expect
  to work. An explicit override is deliberately **not** clamped: the installer picked
  that day on purpose, so a conflict is surfaced as the warning rather than silently
  overruled.
- **`expireStaleLocks` clears any pending `lockedDate` before the plan day**, from the
  top of `refreshPlanDay` so both the anchor and Optimize see a solvable list. It has
  to run *before* the items are read — an `allSorted()` taken before the sweep is
  already stale, which is a mistake worth only making once.

A missed **appointment** (dated in the past) stays an error rather than joining the
lock sweep. A lock is a routing convenience whose day has gone; an appointment is a

## The meters/day target has a ceiling, and nothing ever said so

## "I set it to 20 and it isn't saving" — and the route was already using 20

The end of a long chase. Three rounds of this were spent on day *sizing* when the

The persistence is four lines, and the write hangs off `change` alone:

```js
$('wlTarget').onchange = () => { … store.set('wlTarget', String(v)); };
```

`change` fires only on **blur**. `targetVal()`, meanwhile, reads the **live field**.
So an installer who typed a new number and then backgrounded the app — or left the

a box that said the old one again on the next open. Reproduced in the real page by
dispatching `input` without `change`:

```
typed 20, then blurred    ->  field 20, stored 20
```

rewriting the field mid-keystroke fights the typing; the trade is that a partial
number can be stored if the app is abandoned between keystrokes, which leaves a
visible wrong number rather than silently discarding the edit.

**`wlPace` has the identical shape** — `onchange` only — and is deliberately left
alone: its handler also sets `paceSource: 'override'`, and firing that per keystroke

## The target only worked downwards — and most of the report was already fixed

things at once and only one of them was still broken:

> When I'm planning my day for tomorrow, knowing that I have a timed appointment, I

> I'm just trying to set up tomorrow. After playing around I notice that I can't even

> stuck on a different value and never got updated. That's what the 20 count was.

Read against the shipped code it splits four ways, and three of them had already gone
out earlier the same day:

| what they saw | why | fixed in |
|---|---|---|
| the appointment shoved onto its own day | `routeStartDate` was always today, so a tomorrow appointment was `workdayOffset` 1 → **Day 2 by construction** | `b70c7dd` |
| a 5pm route full of *today's* orders | same line — Day 1 was a day that was already over | `b70c7dd` |
| the day count frozen | every existing `lockedDate` carried the old plan day, so `scheduleRouteConstraints` **threw** on every Optimize, aborting before the `day` tags *and* the geometry were written | `f613c44` |
| the box showing 24 after setting 20 | the store write hung off `change` while `targetVal()` reads the live field | `466cdb2` |

Saying so plainly matters more than it looks: three of the four symptoms share a
single sentence in the report, and a fix aimed at that sentence today would have been
a fix for something already gone.

**What survived all of it is one asymmetry.** Day 1 is sized
`min(dayCapacity(target, installedToday) + anchor.extend, anchor.ids.length)`.
Lowering the target clamps the day down at once, through `capacity`. Raising it does
nothing — the frozen anchor set is re-committed only when `needsCommit` sees `replan`
**and** a changed target, and until now the only caller passing `replan` was an
explicit Optimize. So on the target box alone, "they get stuck on a different value
and never got updated" was literally true, in one direction, for every increase.

That exclusion was a deliberate decision, taken this morning in *A Day 1 frozen at 6
meters/day stayed six orders forever* higher up this page: typing is not a re-plan,
the press is, and the press is also the moment the `day` tags have just been re-solved
at the new target so the fresh commit reads tags that match it. The reasoning holds
and the conclusion was still wrong, because it
assumed the installer would reach for Optimize afterwards — and the guide said to.
What they actually reach for is the number, and a control that answers to one
direction reads as broken rather than as awaiting a second press.

So `$('wlTarget').onchange` now runs `applyTodayAnchor({ replan: true, resize: true })`,
then re-renders and re-advances plan mode. Four things about doing it that way:

- **`replan` alone fixed nothing, and that was caught by simulating it rather than
  reading it.** `freshAnchorIds` deliberately prefers the `day` tags already on the
  orders — which is right after an Optimize, since it re-solved them at the new target
  moments earlier. From the box nothing has re-solved them, so they still describe the
  **old** target, and the re-commit dutifully froze the same undersized group: target
  8 → 14 still produced a Day 1 of 8. Run against the pure helpers before and after:

  ```
  target 8 -> 14   replan only          Day 1 = 8    <- still stuck
  target 8 -> 14   replan + resize      Day 1 = 14
  target 8 ->  4   either               Day 1 = 4    (capacity always clamped down)
  mid-day, 6 in, 8 -> 20                Day 1 = 14   = the capacity left, not a fresh 20
  ```

  `resize` sets `freshAnchorIds`' new `opts.fromTags: false`, taking the set by count
  instead. It defaults **true**, so every existing caller keeps the tag preference
  untouched. Nothing timed is lost by ignoring the tags: `scheduleRouteConstraints`
  re-imposes appointments and locks immediately afterwards.
- **It reuses `replan` rather than adding a second unfreeze path.** Everything that
  makes unfreezing safe already hangs off that flag: `freeReplan` (a day with no
  installs *and* no UTIs, or one already closed out, is a day nobody has driven — it
  reshuffles freely), `midReplan` bounding a started day to the `dayCapacity` it has
  left, and the `capacity === 0` skip that refuses to commit an empty set. A parallel
  route into the anchor would have had to re-derive all three, and the ways to get one
  of them wrong are documented above at some length.
- **The box passes `replan` without `travel`; Optimize passes both.** That single
  argument is now the whole difference between the two callers. With no matrix in hand
  the re-split falls back to `estimateTravel` over the saved pins, so the times come
  back honest but softer — `wlTimesEstimated` is set and the route view labels them
  **(est.)**. Same split, estimated clock. Optimize remains the thing that buys exact
  ETAs, which is the honest division of labour rather than a limitation.
- **`change`, not `input`.** The days must not re-split on the keystroke between 2 and
  20. Persistence stays on `input` (that is the previous entry's fix and it is
  untouched); only the *settled* value re-plans.

**And `wlPace` finally gets the same treatment.** The last entry recorded that it had
the identical lost-edit shape and was being left alone on purpose, because its handler
also stamps `paceSource: 'override'` and `refreshAvgDay` refuses to overwrite an
override. Firing that per keystroke would pin an override on a half-typed number.

The knot unties by giving the override an exit. `oninput` persists — guarded to a
finite positive number, so a mid-clear blank never writes over a real value — and a
**cleared box on `change` reverts** to the measured 30-workday pace and drops the flag,
repainting the same hint `refreshAvgDay` would. That mirrors the on-site override box,
whose "leave it empty to go back to the measured number" is already in the guide, and
it is what makes the `input` write safe rather than merely convenient: without a way
back, a pace pinned as an override is pinned on that device forever.

Worth noticing about the old behaviour on the way past: blanking the box used to land
`paceMin` on the flat `30` fallback *flagged as an override*, so the installer's real
measured pace could never return. Nobody reported it, which is what you would expect
from a number that is only slightly wrong.

Phone-local, no schema change, no spine change, nothing new in `SHELL`. Three new tests
(441 → 444), the third being the pure one that would have caught `replan`-alone on its
own: `freshAnchorIds` honours the day tags by default and takes the set by count under
`fromTags: false`, with `opts.max` still binding. That test exists because reading the
code said the fix worked and running it said otherwise.

Driven in headless Edge as well as asserted, on the reported scenario rather than a
convenient one — evening clock, 22 pending orders, one **locked to today** (the state

```
B. raise 8 -> 14, no press days 14/8     appointment still Day 1 09:40
```

## The day divider stopped guessing how long a day is

`js/route-constraints.js`, `js/worklist.js`, `js/route-variants.js`,
`tests/route-constraints.test.mjs`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `USER-GUIDE.md`

Asked in passing — "is that ETA factoring in the road time and the installer's average
time per stop?" — and the honest answer was *one of the two numbers is, the other never
was*.

The per-stop **ETA** badge has both halves: `simulateDay` walks `arrival = previous
departure + drive`, `departure = arrival + on-site`, with the drive from whichever
matrix carried the run and the on-site from the measured dwell model. The **day
divider** beside it did not. It was `count × recent30AvgLogMin + 60` — the installer's
historical log-to-log cadence times the meter count, plus an hour of lunch — which
knows nothing about this route. A tight day and one scattered across the district read
identically, and the km printed two characters to its left disagreed with it.

**`dayDurationMin` reads the schedule back instead of modelling it a second time**: the
departure clock through the last stop's departure (`scheduledEta` + its own
`scheduledOnSiteMin`). One model, so the header and the badges under it cannot drift —
`08:15 → 09:34` on a three-stop day is `~1h 19m` on the divider and the last badge says
`09:09`, which is what the headless run below shows.

Three decisions worth keeping:

- **No lunch hour on the simulated span.** Adding it would make the header announce a
  finish the last ETA badge contradicts, which is the exact failure being removed. The
  old figure keeps its `+60` in its new job as the **fallback** — a list that has never
  been routed has no ETAs to add up, and there the historical cadence is all there is.
  The divider's tooltip says which of the two you are looking at.
- **`scheduledOnSiteMin` now rides with every `scheduledEta` write.** Only Optimize was
  writing it; the today-anchor re-lead (which runs after *every* logged stop), the drag

  optimize behind. Stale by a few minutes rather than wrong, but the route view has been
  printing that number per card since the dwell model landed, and now the day length

- **A day under an hour prints `~25m`, not `~0h 25m`.** The old formula could not land

## An appointment was placed against a day that did not exist — and being late killed the whole route

`js/route-constraints.js`, `js/route-variants.js`, `js/worklist.js`,

`tests/route-constraints.test.mjs`, `tests/route-variants.test.mjs`,
`tests/route-late-surfacing.test.mjs`

Reported with two screenshots of the same moment: after an Optimize, a toast said
*"WO 693867 would arrive at 10:39, after 10:00"*, the plan-date hint said the same order
would arrive at **10:13**, and the route map showed it at **ETA 09:55** — on time.
Three numbers, one order. The report's own conclusion was the right one: *"this bug makes
me doubt the ETA listed on the map view."*

**All three were real, and none of them were the same computation.**

| surface | number | what it was |
|---|---|---|
| toast | 10:39 | thrown out of `optimizeRouteHandler`'s variant loop with the run's **real road matrix** — and the throw lands before every write, so the Optimize was abandoned whole |
| plan-date hint | 10:13 | parked in `wlPlanIssue` by `applyTodayAnchor`'s catch, which re-runs the same solve with `estimateTravel` — crow-flies haversine, so a different number |
| route map | 09:55 | `scheduledEta` from the last schedule that **succeeded**, with nothing marking it stale |

Every failure path is deliberately designed to leave the last good schedule in place. That
is still right — a stale route beats no route in a truck. What was missing is that
**nothing said it had happened**, so the ETA the crew navigates by silently belonged to a
route the solver had since rejected.

### Why it failed at all: the search priced a day of placeholders

`placeAppointments` picks an appointment's slot by simulating the day, then
`scheduleRouteConstraints` re-simulates the winner for real. Those were **not the same
day**. The search padded every non-appointment slot with a `__free_k` placeholder, and
`travelLookup` has no matrix row for a placeholder — so `simulateDay` charged the nominal
`pace − onSite` fallback between them (10 minutes) and, when slot 1 was a placeholder,
**zero for the drive out of the muster point**. A day of 30-minute legs was searched as a
day of 10-minute ones. A slot that looked comfortably on time arrived an hour late for
real, which threw and took the route down — while an on-time arrangement sat unconsidered.

Reproduced on the shipped commit, four stops, 30 out and 30 between, one 10:00
appointment:

```
scheduleRouteConstraints(...)          → THREW: WO APPT would arrive at 11:00, after 10:00
the same day, appointment locked first → eta 09:40, wait 70
```

The fix hands each day's **real** free orders into the search, so the slot it picks is the
slot the final simulation will actually walk. The trap to remember: any second model of a
day is a model that can disagree with the first one, and this one disagreed silently in
the optimistic direction, which is the direction that throws later instead of sooner.

### Never late, only early

The standing rule from the crew, in their words: *"arriving late is not allowed, even if
the route is out of the way and the installer would sit there for 30/45 mins — that is the
acceptable option, never late only early."* Two halves:

- **An on-time arrangement always beats a late one**, whatever the wait it costs. Among
  on-time arrangements the least waiting still wins (the latest non-late slot), so a day
  that *can* stay productive still does — waiting is what you accept to avoid lateness,
  not something to seek out.
- **Lateness is reported, never thrown.** `simulateDay` records `lateMin` on the stop that
  misses instead of pushing a string into `errors`; `errors` is now only for input that
  cannot be scheduled at all. An appointment no slot can reach — an 08:15 promise 90
  minutes from an 08:00 departure — takes the earliest slot available and carries a red
  **⚠ 39m late** badge on its card, the route view and the planner row. Losing twenty-three
  other orders to one impossible one was never the right trade, and the crew is better
  placed than the solver to decide what to do about it.

### One fact drives every surface

`wlPlanIssue` is now the single staleness signal, and an Optimize whose solve fails parks
it too instead of only toasting. While it is set, the plan-date hint carries the reason,
the route view's header reads **"⚠ Times below are from an earlier route — re-optimize"**,
and every ETA badge on both screens is greyed and struck through. They cannot disagree the
way the map's 09:55 and the worklist's 10:13 did, because there is only one thing for them
to read.
