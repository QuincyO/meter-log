# 2026-11-23 — The audit PDF's route map is zoomed to the orders; a day's audit is something to skim; a typed town is found from the streets around it; Clear map pins; the route map names the orders sharing a pin; one address can be looked up online

## The route map in the PDF fits the orders alone

**The problem:** the map under **Route worked** in a day's audit PDF was zoomed to hold
everything drawn on it: the orders, the line between them and, with **Show drive log**
ticked, the whole drive. A drive out from the start location zoomed it out until the
numbered pins bunched together and the order worked could not be read.

**What shipped:** the box is fitted to the orders' pins and nothing else. The drive log and
the road line are still drawn, and are cut off at the edge of the box where they go
further. The street map behind it covers the same area.

**Traps worth remembering:**

- The fit and the clip go together. A line that leaves the box is drawn over the page's
  text unless it is clipped.
- The drawer and the street-map fetch must read the same points (`mapParts`), or the pins
  and the picture drift apart.
- The map on the audit page is not changed. Ticking **Show drive log** there still zooms
  out to the drive.

## A day's audit is something to skim, with the reasons on the gaps

**The ask, from the owner after reading his PDF:** it was too many words, and technical
ones. A supervisor should be able to look at the charts, read the headlines and a few
numbers, and know how the day went. His notes should sit on the gaps they explain, and
every gap should carry its work order number.

**What shipped**, on the day view of both audit pages and in its PDF:

- **The day at a glance:** six tiles, the comment typed at End of day, and one caption.
- **Where the time went:** a row for each gap that ran long or had something entered on
  it, with the order number, the minutes over usual, what was entered, and the order's
  note or UTI reason under it.
- **Every order:** shorter, with the UTI reason in the status and any note not already
  shown above.
- How the figures are worked out is four closed sections at the bottom of the page. They
  are not in the PDF. A 33-order day went from four pages to two.
- The PDF no longer has speeds. The page keeps them.
- "Longest pause" names its order on the period view too. Otherwise the period view is
  unchanged.

**The read carries more:** `?action=audit` now returns order notes, UTI reasons and End of
day comments. It is token-only. The notes were already readable that way through `day`; the
comment was only in the gated `tracker` read, and the owner was told before it was added.

**Traps worth remembering:**

- The comment is on the Tracker row, so it exists only after the day is closed.
- Notes print as typed. Anything in one goes to whoever is sent the PDF.
- No number changed: the out-of-repo figure check still reads 0 DIFF.
- The expected number keeps its conditions on screen: whose pace it is, that it is not a
  target, and how close it has been.

## A typed town is found from the streets around it

**The report, from the owner:** six orders typed `6 York St, Delaware, ON N0L 1E0` (and
16, 15, 14, 10, 8) were put on York St in London. He was in London when he pressed
Optimize.

**The cause:** the downloaded map tags each address point with whatever town the mapper
wrote. York St's points in Delaware say "Middlesex Centre", the municipality. Gideon Dr
and Longwoods Rd, beside it, say "Delaware". The lookup read the town only from the
candidate road's own points, found no York St tagged Delaware, and dropped the typed town.
That left a tie between 19 York Streets, and a tie goes to the one clearly nearest the
phone: London's. The saved route had a 19.4 km leg out to the first York St order.

**What shipped:** when no candidate road carries the typed town, the lookup finds that
town's points on other streets and keeps the candidates within 3 km of one
(`besideTown`, `TOWN_NEAR_M` in `js/roadgraph.js`). York St in Delaware is 90 m from a
point tagged Delaware; York St in London is 13 km from one. Run against the published
district with the owner's 117 orders: the six York St orders move from London to
Delaware and the other 110 lookups are unchanged.

**Traps worth remembering:**

- The postal code is not read. The pack holds none, so `N0L 1E0` on the end neither
  helps nor hurts. The town is what narrows it.
- It runs only when no candidate carries the tag itself, so a road that is tagged with
  the typed town is found exactly as before.
- With no town typed, a tie goes to the street nearest the phone, not nearest the rest
  of the list. That is the owner's rule, confirmed on 2026-11-23. The user guide said
  "nearest your other orders" and now says "nearest your phone".
- An order already pinned in the wrong town is not looked up again, because its pin is
  inside the radius. Edit the address and save it to clear the pin, then Optimize.
- After a wrong pin, the town-writing pass puts that pin's town on the address
  (`confirmFoundAddresses`): a wrong pin in London rewrites "Delaware" as "London".
- The 3 km comes from the published district: of the streets in a small town, 89% lie
  within 3 km of another street's point with the same tag, and 95% within 5 km.

**Also looked at, nothing changed in the app:** holding Optimize showed an error. Google's
Routes service answered every call with `403 PERMISSION_DENIED — Permission denied on the
service.` A geocode on the same key worked. The Routes API was not enabled on the
project; the owner enabled it the same day and a test call then returned a route
(DEPLOY.md, the optimize toast entry). With 85 orders the OpenRouteService backup is over
its size limit, so while Google refused, the hold fell to straight lines.

## 📍 Clear map pins

**The ask, from the owner:** a way to delete all the pins on the orders. A pin set in the
wrong town could not be fixed quickly in the field.

**Why it was slow:** a pin inside the radius is never looked up again. The only way to
drop one was to retype that order's address.

**What shipped:** a **📍 Clear map pins (N)** button near the bottom of the worklist. After
a confirm it removes the pin and the parked flags from every order still to do. The next
Optimize looks each address up again from where the phone is.

**Traps worth remembering:**

- Clearing the pin alone would not have fixed York St. After a wrong pin the app writes
  that pin's town onto the address, so "6 York St, Delaware" read "6 York St, London" and
  the next lookup would find London again. The app now keeps the text it writes a town
  over (`typedAddress`) and a clear puts it back.
- A clear never takes the town off an address. See the next section: the first version
  did, and it made the owner's list worse.
- An order that was never pinned keeps its text. A pin typed as coordinates and a logged
  order are never cleared.
- `typedAddress` stays on the phone. A list download would wipe it, so the download
  carries it over for each order whose address comes back unchanged.
- The town-writing pass runs behind Optimize. It now checks the order still has the same
  pin before it writes, or a clear pressed in that moment would get the old town back.
- Nothing is uploaded. Until the next log or ⇪ Upload, the Drive screen's refresh can put
  the sheet's pins back.
- After a clear, an address the downloaded map does not have is set aside on a tap. Hold
  Optimize to look those up online.

## Clear map pins took the town off every address; a street far from its town is a miss

**The report, from the owner, an hour after the button shipped:** "York St is still getting
set to London, and now there's another address that's getting set to Milton when it's in
Komoka. Overall this is a worse result."

**What happened:** as first shipped (`41fe259`), a clear took the town off any pinned order
that had no typed copy saved, so the next lookup would take the street nearest the phone.
No order had a typed copy yet. The owner pressed it in London with 85 orders. The uploaded
list showed eight in the wrong place afterwards: six York St orders and 95 Wellington St in
London, and 33 Caverhill Cres in Milton, 145 km away. Replaying the clear on the earlier copy
of the list against the published district gave the same Wellington and Caverhill results.

**What shipped:**

- **A clear never takes the town off an address.** With no typed copy the address is left
  as it is. A typed copy that is only the number and street is not put back, so the 80
  town-less copies the first version left on the owner's phone do nothing.
- **A street a long way from the typed town is a miss.** The map has no Caverhill Cres in
  Komoka, only one in Milton. With any town typed the lookup ignored the town and answered
  Milton; a cached Google pin had been hiding that. A road more than 25 km from a town the
  map can place is now dropped (`TOWN_FAR_M`). The order is set aside, and a hold looks it
  up online.

Replayed on the list as uploaded: a clear followed by a lookup from London now leaves all 82
lookups where they are. On the earlier copy of the list: 81 land within 1 km of their old
pin, none move, and Caverhill is set aside.

**Traps worth remembering:**

- The idea behind the first version was "the installer is standing in the work area, so the
  nearest street is the right one". The owner plans from London. Don't let the phone's
  position choose a town for an order that already names one.
- The first version was checked in a browser with the phone placed in Delaware. It was
  never run from anywhere else, and from anywhere else was the case that mattered.
- A clear also replaces Google's pins with the offline map's. On this list they moved by
  up to about 100 m. An address the map does not have loses its pin until a hold.
- The eight orders already wrong are not mended by this. Their addresses now say the wrong
  town, so they have to be retyped or put back from a copy of the list.

## The route map names the orders sharing a pin, and its button opens them

**The report, from the owner, with a screenshot of the All tab:** the note said "4 sharing
a pin with a different address" and the button under it said **Fix pins or optimize**. The
button went back to the worklist, which marks none of the four. "I would like to see which
orders are sharing a pin and then the button should allow me to review the problematic
addresses."

**What shipped:**

- **The note lists the orders, pin by pin**: address, work order, day and stop number.
  A pin whose orders are on two days is listed whole from either day's tab.
- **Tapping one opens the address screen at that order.** The button does the same from
  the first one, and reads **Check these 4 addresses**. The screen is the "Fill in
  missing addresses" walkthrough with a queue the route map picked: the shared pins, then
  any parked or blank order on screen. Its heading is **Check addresses** and its back
  button is **‹ Route**.
- **Each order says why it is there**: which other address is on its pin, and what moves
  the pin.
- **Looking changes nothing.** Back, Skip and an unedited Save leave the list, the order
  and the schedule as they were.
- With only unpinned orders in the note, the button is unchanged: back to the worklist.

**The traps:**

- **Retyping the same address gets the same pin back.** On the owner's list the four were
  39 and 41 Pleasant St (the map has 39 and put 41 on it) and 8 and 95 Wellington St (the
  map has neither number and put both on the same point). A hold on Optimize does not
  help either: the offline answer comes first even on a hold, so Google is never asked.
  The note therefore says to correct the address or type coordinates. Typed coordinates
  replace the address text until the next Optimize writes the nearest address over them,
  and that may be the neighbour's number. An online lookup for one order is the better
  fix, and it shipped next (below).
- **An order that only lacks a pin is left out of the queue.** After Clear map pins that
  is every order.
- **`onDone` is skipped when a review changed nothing.** It re-writes the list order and,
  with a locked stop, re-runs the scheduler.
- There is no way to say "this pin is fine". 39 and 41 Pleasant St are next door and the
  note will keep naming them.

**Verification:** `node --test "tests/*.test.mjs"` — 1,282/1,282. Driven in headless Edge
with the spine blocked, on the owner's 84 pending orders as they stood on the sheet:

| Step | Seen |
| --- | --- |
| All tab | the note, two pins of two orders each, button "Check these 4 addresses" |
| Tap 95 Wellington St | "Check addresses", "4 of 4", the note naming 8 Wellington St |
| Phone Back | the map, still on All, every row in IndexedDB unchanged |
| The button, Skip × 3 | 39 Pleasant, 41 Pleasant, 8 Wellington, 95 Wellington, each naming the other |
| Coordinates in Street, Save & finish | that one order re-pinned, no other row changed, the note down to 2 |
| Uploads | none |

Not checked: a real phone.

## 🌐 Look up online: one address, asked of Google, from the address screen

**Why:** the review above could name a bad pin and not mend it. The offline map answers
first everywhere, even on a hold, so nothing the installer could press reached Google for
an address the map answers badly. The owner asked for the lookup the same evening.

**What shipped:** a **🌐 Look up online** button on the address screen, under the address
boxes. It is on both uses of that screen: the route map's review and "Fill in missing
addresses".

- It asks Google about what is in the boxes, so a corrected number can be checked before
  it is saved.
- It shows up to three answers, nearest the present pin first. Each says how far it is
  from that pin and whether Google knows the building or only the street.
- Tapping an answer moves the pin and goes to the next order. Asking writes nothing.
- The address text stays as typed. Google's wording is kept beside it.

**The traps:**

- **It chooses nothing.** After the town mistakes earlier today, the installer sees the
  town and the distance and decides.
- **A street-only answer is marked ⚠.** Google answers an unknown number with the middle
  of the street, and that is not the house.
- **One Geocoding request per press, no road matrix.** The phone's monthly road-distance
  allowance is not touched.
- **Clear map pins drops this pin** and the offline map answers it again as before. It is
  not an `exactPin`: that flag means the address text is not where the order is.
- `geocodeOne` is unchanged. A tap is offline and a hold takes the offline answer first.

**Verification:** `node --test "tests/*.test.mjs"` — 1,286/1,286. Driven in headless Edge
on the owner's list with the spine blocked and only the two geocoding requests let out:

| Step | Seen |
| --- | --- |
| Review 8 Wellington St, Look up online | one answer, "The building itself · 960 m from the pin it has now"; nothing written |
| Skip to 95 Wellington St | the last answer is gone |
| Look up online, tap the answer | "The building itself · 150 m"; that order re-pinned, address text unchanged, no other row changed |
| Back on the map | the note down to the two Pleasant St orders |
| Uploads | none |

So on this list the offline map had 8 Wellington St nearly a kilometre out. Not checked: a
real phone, an address with several answers against the real service (covered by a test
with a stubbed answer), and no signal.

## Looked at, not changed: Day 1 passed Gideon Dr and York St

**The question, same message:** Day 1 ran along Longwoods Rd and left the orders on Gideon
Dr and York St, right beside it, for Day 2. "Doesn't it make more sense to get those since
we're passing by it already?"

**What was measured**, replaying the split on the 84 orders over the published district
(the replay put all 84 on the day the phone shows):

- The day holds 32. Eleven went on the way in (Troops Rd, Adelaide Rd, Clark St), which
  left 21 places. The corner had 33 orders: 14 on Longwoods, 7 on John St, 3 on Gideon,
  6 on York and 3 at 11548 Longwoods.
- The walk took the 14 on Longwoods, each a few metres from the last. At the east end the
  next stop was 1 John St at 79 m or back to 11548 Longwoods at 95 m. John St won, its
  seven orders filled the day, and Gideon (3), York (6) and 11548 Longwoods (3) went to
  Day 2.
- Day 1 with Gideon, 11548 Longwoods and one York St order in place of John St is 3.81 km
  between stops against 3.70 km. York St still does not fit whole.

So the rule did what it says (fewest metres for Day 1) on a difference of 110 m, and it
has no notion of a street being finished. `pickDay` was not changed: the 2026-11-10 note
in `route.js` records that insertion made Day 1 longer about as often as shorter on random
lists, and one list is not grounds to change it.

**Then tested, at the owner's request ("I don't think it will be [worth it] but I want to be
sure"), and it is not worth it.** 28 real lists were planned each way on the published
district, at 24 and at 32 a day (56 plans): the owner's list, pending and whole, and 26
past weeks of two installers rebuilt from the Stops tab (59 to 124 orders each, at the
position each stop was logged from). "Passed" counts orders left for a later day within
120 m of a stop an earlier day drove by, at least 100 m before that day ended.

| Rule | Orders passed | Day 1 | All days | Between stops | Worst Day 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| As shipped | 432 | — | — | — | — |
| Insertion always (`fitDay`) | 460 | +0.8% | +0.2% | −0.2% | +24% |
| Insertion when within 5% of the walk | 462 | +0.3% | +0.1% | −0.2% | +4% |
| Sweep: take a passed order, give up the day's last stop | 574 | −0.5% | +1.4% | +5.9% | +5% |
| The same sweep on Day 1 only | 548 | −0.5% | +1.3% | +4.6% | +5% |

The sweep is the rule the owner described. It does what was asked on Day 1 of the owner's
list (Gideon and 11548 Longwoods come in, John St goes out) and leaves **more** orders
passed overall, because the stops a day gives up at its far end become a pocket that a
later day drives by. Day-1-only sweep: fewer passed in 10 plans, more in 15, the same in
31. No rule tried left fewer than the shipped one. `pickDay` stays as it is.

Limits of the test: the past weeks are orders as they were worked, not as they were
pending; lists away from the crew's meet were given a start 12 km west of their middle;
and these are four ways to write the rule, not every way.
