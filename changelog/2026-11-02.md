# 2026-11-02 — Fill in addresses: a second Save, a chip for every street; a slot-1 lock starts the route

## A Save under Back / Skip as well as in the header

On [2026-10-30](2026-10-30.md), **Save & next** moved into the walkthrough's header, so
the keyboard can't cover it. The owner asked to keep a Save in its old spot too, under
Back / Skip, for when the keyboard is down.

**What shipped:** `#wlAddrSaveBottom` in `index.html`, bound to the same `save()` in
`worklist-address-fill.js`. `paint()` gives it the header button's label, so on the last
order both buttons say "Save & finish".

**The trap:** it's a new id, so the HTML and JS can reach a phone one update apart
(AGENTS.md §"CROSS-FILE CONTRACT"). The button ships with `hide`, and the module removes
it only once it has bound the click. On a phone with the old module and the new page, the
button stays hidden instead of doing nothing. On a phone with the new module and the old
page, the `if(saveBottom)` guard skips it.

**Verification:** a new test in `tests/worklist-address-fill.test.mjs` checks that the
button is hidden in the markup, sits under Back / Skip, and is bound with the guard.
`node --test "tests/*.test.mjs"` passes (768). A headless Edge drive at 390 px seeded
three blank-address orders. Tapping the bottom Save saved `14 Holden Ave, Simcoe` and
went to 2 of 3. Tapping the header Save saved the next one and went to 3 of 3, with both
labels on "Save & finish". The drive blocked all spine calls, and no POST was attempted.

## A chip for every street, not just six

The walkthrough's street chips stopped at 6, the default `limit` of `recentStreets`.
A list built from WO#s alone gets filled in street by street, so once a 7th street had
been typed, the oldest street lost its chip and had to be typed out again.

**What shipped:** the walkthrough calls `recentStreets(items, Infinity)`, which gives a
chip for every distinct street on the list, still most recent first. The Add order /
Edit form keeps its 6, as the owner chose.

**The trap:** the chips sit *above* the fields so the keyboard can't cover them (see
[2026-10-30](2026-10-30.md)). An unlimited set wrapping onto as many rows as it needs
would push the fields down under the keyboard again. `#wlAddrChips` is therefore capped
at 140 px, three rows of 36 px chips plus a sliver of a fourth that shows the box
scrolls. `overscroll-behavior:contain` stops a swipe inside the box from scrolling the
page, and `paintChips` resets the box to the top for each order, where the newest
street is.

**Verification:** a new test checks that the default stays 6, that the walkthrough asks
for every street, and that the box scrolls. `node --test "tests/*.test.mjs"` passes
(769). A headless Edge drive at 390 px seeded 15 streets and showed all 15 chips in the
140 px box (344 px of content). Scrolling to the bottom chip and tapping it filled the
Street box. After saving, that street came first. The drive blocked all spine calls, and
no POST was attempted.

## A stop locked to slot 1 is where the route starts

**The report:** the installer locked 114 Brook St to slot 1 of Day 1, with a lot of
other Brook St orders beside it, and pressed Optimize. The road route kept most of the
street but skipped some, and put the farthest one 2nd. The straight-line route skipped
Brook St completely. What they wanted: the 2nd stop is the nearest to the 1st, the 3rd
nearest to the 2nd, and so on.

**Cause:** the solver never saw the lock. `solveVariant` ordered every pending stop
furthest-from-home first, then cut that into days. Only afterwards did
`scheduleRouteConstraints` drop the lock into slot 1 and fill slots 2..10 with the first
free stops of that sequence, which had been picked for being far from home. Whether
Brook St landed in Day 1 was luck, and the two matrices had different luck. This wasn't
a regression: locks were a scheduling-only layer from the start
([2026-07-22](2026-07-22.md)).

**What shipped:** the lead. `route-constraints.js routeLeadId` finds the pending stop
locked to Day 1 slot 1. Both Optimize handlers (`worklist.js`, `pages/planner.js`) pass
it to `optimizeRoute` as `opts.leadId`. `route.js solveWithLead` grows Day 1 from it:
a nearest-neighbour walk picks the day's `target` stops, then the existing anchored
2-opt/Or-opt orders them with the lead first and the end bent home by the commute pull.
Days 2+ are the unchanged solve over what's left. The lead also beats a GPS "start from
here" fix for the first stop. The fix stays in the matrix, so the drive to the lead is
still timed and priced. Both variants go through `solveVariant`, so road and
straight-line both get it. Locks on other slots work as before.

**The trap:** a slot-1 lock has to reach the solver. Any new Optimize caller that skips
`leadId` brings the bug back with no error (AGENTS.md has the bullet). The pages reach
`routeLeadId` through a namespace import with a `typeof` check, so a phone holding an
old cached `route-constraints.js` loses the lead, not the page.

**Not fixed here:** in the same screenshots the locked card read "9105004m on site"
while its neighbours read 13m. The ETAs show the scheduler charged 13 min, so it's a
stray value on that one stored order, not something the solver produced. It needs that
order's record from the phone (VERIFY.md §6) before anything changes.

**Verification:** new `tests/route-lead.test.mjs` (14 tests). Its fixture is a street of
11 houses beside the lock, 12 orders across town and home just past the street. Without
a lead it reproduces the report: Day 1 holds at most 2 street stops. With the lead,
Day 1 is `L, s1…s9` on straight-line, and the same set with `L` first on a perturbed
road matrix. It also runs the real `optimizeRoute` → `scheduleRouteConstraints`
pipeline (straight-line tap, and a stubbed OSRM run with both variants). Against the
old `route.js`, 7 of the new tests fail. `node --test "tests/*.test.mjs"` passes (783).
A headless Edge load of `index.html` with the spine stubbed shows no exceptions, and the
worklist screen renders. A real Optimize tap on a road pack was not driven.
