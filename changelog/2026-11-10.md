# 2026-11-10 — Orders beside you are no longer left for tomorrow

## A day is the shorter of two builds, not one walk

**The report:** a locked Optimize "from where I am" in Sombra, target 20 with one install
logged, so Day 1 held 19. The route took 108 and 114 Middle St, then ran south down Duke
St and on to Smith, Kenzie and John St. Three orders on the north end of Duke St — 120
Middle St, 3450 and 3451 Duke St, a few doors from where the crew stood — were not on
Day 1 at all. They were tomorrow's first three stops. Straight-line was wrong the other
way round: it took those three and left 108 Middle St, 40 m from the start, for Day 2.

**The cause:** `pickDay` chose a day's stops with a nearest-neighbour walk and nothing
else, and a walk never looks back. Leaving 108 Middle St it had a near tie: 3461 Duke St
to the south at 108 m by road, 120 Middle St to the north at 112 m. South won by 4 m.
From there every step found something closer ahead, the three northern orders fell
further behind with each one (about 500 m by the last pick), and the day filled at 19.
Nothing decided those orders belonged to tomorrow; they were what was left when the
count ran out. The `Worklist` tab showed `day` equal to `dayRoad` on every row, so the
router itself produced the split, and replaying the solver on the saved pins gave the
sheet's Day 1 set exactly, for both variants.

**What shipped:** `pickDay` now builds the day twice and keeps the shorter. `walkDay` is
the old walk, unchanged. `fitDay` is cheapest insertion: each stop is added wherever it
lengthens the path least, which may be in the middle of it, so a pocket beside the route
is picked up on the way past. Both sets are ordered the way the day will be driven
(`dayLength` → `orderDay`) and the shorter one wins; a tie keeps the walk.

On the reported list the road route's Day 1 becomes 108 Middle, 114 Middle, 120 Middle,
3450 Duke, 3451 Duke, then down Duke St as before. Between-stop driving goes from 849 m
to 823 m on Day 1 and from 1016 m to 658 m on Day 2, which no longer opens with a return
trip.

**The trap worth remembering — two of them:**

- **Insertion is not the better builder, so it must not replace the walk.** Measured over
  300 random lists each of village, rural and scattered layouts, insertion alone made
  Day 1 longer about as often as shorter, with worst cases 50–60% longer. Keeping the walk
  as a candidate is what guarantees a day never comes out longer than it did before.
- **A pairwise swap search cannot find this.** Swapping one pocket stop for the day's last
  stop costs the whole detour and saves one leg, so no single swap improves; only the
  pocket as a group does. That is why the swap search measured on 2026-11-05 changed
  Day 1 in no case and was not shipped.

**What it does not do:** the goal is still that today finishes soonest, not that no
neighbour is ever left behind. Where the walk's day is already the shorter one it stands.
On this same list the straight-line variant is unchanged — in crow-flies metres its
existing Day 1 is the shorter day, so 108 Middle St still lands on Day 2 there.

**Verified:** the new tests fail on the previous solver and pass on this one; the full
suite passes (863). The report was replayed in Node against the installer's saved pins
and the published district pack, before and after, and the changed module was loaded in
headless Edge and run on the pocket case. The Optimize button itself was not pressed in
a browser — the change is inside the pure solver and adds no export, import or markup.
