# 2026-07-26 — The phone measures its own roads; Optimize stops eating scrolls

## A progress bar for district builds, and the "out of province" failure explained

`tools/roadpack-server.mjs`, `tools/build-roadpack.mjs`, `js/districts.js`,
`js/pages/planner.js`, `planner.html`, `css/planner.css`, `tests/districts.test.mjs`,
`AGENTS.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `DEPLOY.md`

Two complaints about the same screen: a build that gives no sign it is working, and a
build that "sometimes says out of province".

**The second one was a real bug, and not about the province at all.** Leaflet reports
**unwrapped** longitudes. Pan the planner map sideways onto the next copy of the world —
easy to do on a wide monitor without noticing — and every coordinate comes back offset by
360, so a rectangle over Parry Sound posts as −440.1 rather than −80.1. osmium rejects
that (`wrong format for coordinate: '-440.0'`, exit 2), and because the step's label is
carried into the error, the message read *"Clipping the district out of the province
failed"*. Hence "sometimes", and hence "out of province": it depended entirely on whether
the map had been panned sideways first. `normalizeBbox` wraps at both ends now — the
planner where the rectangle is made, the service on the way in. Reproduced before the
fix, and rebuilt through it afterwards from the same −440 coordinates.

**Then the rectangle is trimmed rather than failed.** Nothing on screen shows where the
province extract's data stops, so drawing past it is the normal mistake — and clipping
empty ground does *not* fail at the clip. osmium writes an empty extract quite happily
and the build dies a minute later in the pack pass with "No drivable segments found —
wrong input file?", which blames the tool. `/status` now reports the extract's own header
bbox (one cached `osmium fileinfo`, header-only, instant on a 1 GB file), the planner
clamps the drawn box to it and says "Trimmed to the edge of the map data", and a
rectangle with no overlap at all is refused **before** any work starts.

Trimming is necessary but not sufficient, and it is worth knowing why: Geofabrik's
bounding box is a **rectangle around a province that is not one**, so a corner of it sits
over Minnesota, passes the clamp, and still contains no Ontario roads. That case now says
so — "no roads in the extract — the province data does not reach there" — instead of
blaming the input file.

**The progress bar is determinate and weighted.** The service knows its nine phases up
front, so the job carries `phase`/`step`/`steps`/`pct`/`startedAt` and the panel draws a
real bar with the phase name and a running clock. The weighting is the part worth
keeping: the first phase scans the **entire** province extract however small the district
is — measured at 22s of a 28s build — so equal-weight steps left the bar on 1/9 for most
of the build, which looks exactly like the hang the bar exists to disprove. `pct` counts
work *behind* the current phase, so it is honestly 0% through the clip; the moving
stripes on the track are what show it is alive. Inflating the number instead would have
been the easy lie.

One bug found while verifying the failure paths, which is rather the point of exercising
them: **a failed build left its scratch files behind.** The cleanup loop ran after the
last step, so any throw skipped it — and a failure is precisely the build that leaves the
most behind. It is a `finally` now. On the small test district that was 10 files; on a
real one it is hundreds of megabytes accumulating in the OSRM data folder.

Verified against the live builder throughout: a −440 rectangle builds, a rectangle
dragged past the west edge is trimmed and logged, a rectangle inside the header box but
off the province fails with the honest message, one nowhere near is refused instantly,
and a failed build leaves `D:\osrm` clean. `node --test` 339/339.

## Districts can be removed and grown, and a phone picks between the ones it holds

`js/districts.js` (new), `tests/districts.test.mjs` (new), `tools/roadpack-server.mjs`,
`js/pages/planner.js`, `js/roadpack.js`, `js/route.js`, `js/pages/capture.js`,
`css/planner.css`, `sw.js`, `USER-GUIDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `DEPLOY.md`

`maps/` could only ever grow: the planner built and published districts and had no way
to take one back, and a crew's area changing meant a district drawn from scratch. Three
changes, sharing one new pure module.

**✕ Remove** (`POST /remove`) deletes the pack and its catalogue entry. Local until
Publish, which stages the deletion through the same `git add -- maps` that stages a new
one. The slug is validated exactly as `/build` validates it, because this reaches a
filename. **A phone that already downloaded the district keeps it** — the pack is in that
phone's IndexedDB and still routes. Unpublishing is the office tidying its catalogue;
reaching through it to delete a crew's working offline map mid-day would be the worse
failure by a distance. This also exposed a gate that had been backwards from the start:
Publish was disabled when the district list was empty, so removing your *last* district
disabled the button that ships the removal. It hangs on `status.git` alone now.

**⊕ Extend** grows a district instead of replacing it. A district *is* one rectangle, so
extending it means rebuilding the **same id** over the union of its old box and the one
just drawn — `buildPack` already overwrites the pack and replaces the catalogue entry, so
an extend turned out to be a build over more ground and nothing else. The id input locks
while extending, because a changed id quietly builds a *second* district rather than
growing the one on screen. When the two rectangles are far apart the union is mostly land
nobody drives, so past 2× the ground of the areas themselves the panel says so and points
at a separate district — which is a real option now, because:

**The phone picks its district per run.** It could already hold several; it routed on
whichever one was selected in Settings, so a crew working two areas had to remember to
switch. `loadGraph(coords)` now scores every installed district on how many of *this*
run's stops fall inside its bbox and decodes only the winner. Three things there are
load-bearing. Scoring reads the **descriptors** in the localStorage mirror, never a
decoded graph — deciding by decoding each candidate is precisely the cost the
one-graph-at-a-time rule exists to avoid. A run that says nothing (no coordinates yet,
every stop outside every district) falls back to the installer's chosen district rather
than to no map, or a phone with a perfectly good district would decline to the next
matrix provider for want of an opinion. And a pack that fails to decode is filtered out
**before** the scoring, so one corrupt district can't win the run and hide the good one
beside it — which is why `decodeFailed` is a Set now and not a single id.

Stops outside the winning district are not new behaviour: they hit `localGraphMatrix`'s
existing repair pass and price crow-flies, exactly as an out-of-coverage stop always has.

One gap the extend created, closed in the same change: a grown district keeps its id and
its name, so the Settings picker's ✓ read "already installed" forever and the crew would
never have downloaded the bigger map. It compares the catalogue's `builtAt`/`bytes`
against what is on the phone and shows **↻** when they differ.

The arithmetic all three share — `unionBbox`, `unionWaste`, `pickPack` — is
`js/districts.js`: pure, DOM-free and unit-tested, the same shape as `route-variants.js`
and `press-hold.js`, because the planner and the phone both need it and neither should
own it. 18 tests, including the ones that pin the fallbacks: a tie goes to the district
picked by hand, and no-coords must still return a real pack.

**Verified against the live builder**, not just asserted: a throwaway district built
(1,802 nodes), extended east and rebuilt in place over the union (2,068 nodes, one pack
file, one catalogue entry, bbox grown), then removed — with a second remove returning a
clean 404 and a `../../sw` id rejected 400. A real district already in `maps/` was
untouched throughout. `node --test` 328/328.

## Fixed: district builds never worked — a container that doesn't exist, and a `--data` typo

`tools/roadpack-server.mjs`, `js/pages/planner.js`, `css/planner.css`,
`DEPLOY.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `.gitignore`

The first real Build was the thing to watch, and it failed. Two independent faults, both
of which the section below correctly flagged as unexercised:

**There is no official osmium container.** The server ran `ghcr.io/osmcode/osmium-tool`.
That image has never existed — `osmcode` publishes none — so every build died on `denied`
from the registry before osmium ran a single step. It is `iboates/osmium:1.19.0` now: the
maintained community build of the same tool, pinned so a district built today builds the
same way in a year, and overridable with `--image` for the next time a registry image
evaporates. **That image's `ENTRYPOINT` is already `osmium`**, so the argv now starts at
the subcommand (`extract`, not `osmium extract`) — a swap to another image has to check
that first.

**`--data` pointed at a folder that doesn't exist.** The service was launched
`--data C:\osrm`; the Ontario extract lives in `D:\osrm`, the folder already mounted into
the OSRM and Nominatim containers. `findPbf()` returned empty, and the build threw "No
.osm.pbf found in the data folder" — *inside a job*, minutes after a Build button that
looked perfectly enabled. `DEPLOY.md` invited this: step 2 of the OSRM setup said "e.g.
`C:\osrm`" while every other line in the document said `D:\osrm`.

So the failure is now a pre-flight instead of a post-mortem. `/build` rejects a missing
extract before starting a job, and the Districts panel disables **Build** with the reason
— on the button *and* spelled out under the district list, because a disabled button's
`title` tooltip never fires in Chrome, which is where the planner runs. The same change
fixes an older wart: `updateDistrictButtons()` is also called from the draw handlers and
the id/name inputs, which had no status to pass, so drawing a rectangle used to re-enable
Build on a machine with no Docker at all. The last `/status` is cached instead.

**Verified end-to-end this time**, which is the whole point of the entry: a real
`POST /build` over a small Parry Sound bbox ran the full osmium chain and the pack build —
1,491 drivable ways → 1,802 nodes, 2,133 segments, 3,332 addresses on 290 streets, 0.2 MB
— then the test district was removed from `maps/` again. `node --test` 310/310.

One landmine recorded alongside it: `*.osm.pbf` and `_build_*` are gitignored now. The
province extract is ~1 GB, past GitHub's 100 MB per-file limit, and a stray copy had been
downloaded next to `data/` where `git add -A` would have swept it into a commit.

## graphify: an optional code knowledge graph, wired in as a nudge and not a wall

`AGENTS.md`, `.gitignore`, `.claude/settings.json`, `.git/hooks/post-commit`,
`.git/hooks/post-checkout`

[graphify](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify) parses the repo into a queryable
graph — tree-sitter AST for code (local, deterministic, no API key), an LLM pass for prose —
so a "how does X reach Y" question can traverse a subgraph instead of reading whole files.
Installed as a user-level CLI (`uv tool install graphifyy`); what landed *in the repo* is
only the wiring.

Three deliberate choices, because the defaults fought this repo's conventions:

- **The directive lives in `AGENTS.md`, not `CLAUDE.md`.** The installer writes a
  `## graphify` section into `CLAUDE.md` by default, which would have made that file carry
  an instruction of its own for the first time — the exact drift `CLAUDE.md` exists to
  prevent — and would have hidden the rule from Codex. It was moved, and rewritten to say
  when *not* to reach for the graph.
- **No `--strict`.** graphify offers a mode where the first raw file read of a session is
  *denied* until a graph query runs. On a 32k-line repo where plenty of questions are
  answered faster by opening the file, that trades real time for a metric. The
  `PreToolUse` hooks are installed as nudges only.
- **The hook command is a bare `graphify`, not the absolute exe path the installer
  wrote.** `.claude/settings.json` is tracked, so `C:/Users/Quincy/.local/bin/graphify.EXE`
  would have shipped one machine's layout to every clone.

`graphify-out/` is gitignored — it is derived data, and a committed graph goes stale the
moment somebody commits without the hooks. The `post-commit`/`post-checkout` hooks rebuild
it from code automatically; **doc edits are not covered**, so a markdown change needs
`graphify update .` or the graph will keep describing docs that no longer say that.

**Not yet exercised:** no graph has been built. Every claim above is about the wiring, and
the hooks correctly stay silent until `graphify-out/graph.json` exists. Whether the graph
actually beats reading the file on a repo this size is still an open question — the honest
expectation is "sometimes", which is why nothing here blocks anything.

## Offline geocoding, and districts built from the planner map

`js/roadgraph.js`, `js/route.js`, `tools/build-roadpack.mjs`,
`tools/roadpack-server.mjs` (new), `js/pages/planner.js`, `planner.html`,
`js/planner-services.js`, `css/planner.css`

Two follow-ons to the offline road maps below, both closing gaps that work left open.

**Addresses ride in the pack now (format v2).** Forward geocoding was the last part of
Optimize that still needed signal. The pack build already reads the OSM data carrying
`addr:housenumber`/`addr:street`, so the index came almost free: a street dictionary, a
locality dictionary, and house-number points sorted by street and number.
`geocodeAddress` slots into `geocodeOne` as **provider −1**, ahead of Nominatim, Google
and ORS. A miss falls straight through to them, so it's an accelerator and never a hard
dependency, and a roads-only pack stays perfectly valid.

Three things there earn their keep:

- **`normalizeStreet` runs on both sides** — the builder normalizes what OSM stored, the
  phone normalizes what the installer typed — and its positional rule is easy to get
  wrong. Expanding only the *final* word looked right until rural Ontario: "Concession
  Rd 4" and "County Rd 12" put the suffix mid-name and never matched. Expanding *every*
  word turns "St Andrews Rd" (Saint) into "street andrews road". The rule is: suffixes
  expand anywhere except the first word; directions expand anywhere. The test that
  caught this is the one asserting both halves.
- **Missing house numbers interpolate** between their neighbours. OSM rarely has every
  civic number, and "between 410 and 460" puts the pin on the right stretch of a 12 km
  concession instead of at one end of it.
- **One hit per locality, never auto-picked**, so route.js's existing `pickBest` raises
  the "⚠ pick a town" ambiguity rather than guessing between two same-named streets.

**Districts are drawn on the planner map.** Hand-typing bbox corners into PowerShell was
the worst part of making a district. A web page can't run Docker, so a small local helper
(`tools/roadpack-server.mjs`) does — probed like OSRM and Nominatim, and the Districts
panel **hides entirely when it isn't running**, because it's needed to *make* a district,
never to plan a route. Draw a rectangle, name it, Build.

**Publish is a separate, confirmed button.** A push to `main` deploys the whole app, and
phones can only download a district once it's published — they fetch from GitHub Pages,
not from the office PC. So building and deploying stayed two deliberate clicks, and
Publish only ever stages `maps/`.

Three security choices in the helper are posture, not style, and shouldn't be tidied
away: it binds to **127.0.0.1 only**, every child process is `spawn`ed with an **argument
array** rather than a shell string, and the district id is validated against a strict
slug before it reaches a filename or a git command.

**Not verified end-to-end:** Docker Desktop wasn't running on this machine, so the
`osmium` command chain inside a real build is unexercised. Everything either side of it
was — validation, job polling, the pack build, the panel — but the first real Build is
the thing to watch.

## Offline road maps: the phone routes on real roads with no signal

`1b22210` · `js/roadgraph.js` (new), `js/roadpack.js` (new), `tools/build-roadpack.mjs` (new),
`js/route.js`, `js/worklist.js`, `js/idb.js`, `js/pages/capture.js`,
`js/planner-services.js`, `index.html`, `css/capture.css`, `sw.js`, `maps/`

Road-accurate routing lived on the desktop, because only the desktop could reach a road
matrix for free — the planner's local Docker OSRM. On the phone it meant holding 🧭 Optimize
for two seconds to pull the **billable** Google Routes matrix, capped at 9,000 elements a
month per device. A 25-stop day costs ~676 of them, so that was about thirteen road runs a
month, and only with signal.

The ask was "map apps download the offline map and work entirely offline — why can't we?"
It turned out we were closer than it looked: the TSP solve, the multi-day split, the ETAs and
the polyline drawing were **already** on-device. The only thing missing was a way to measure
roads.

So the phone got one. A **district pack** — the drivable road network for one working area,
distilled to node positions, segments with a speed and a oneway bit, and shape points for
drawing — downloads once over wifi from Settings ▸ **Offline road map** and lives in the new
IndexedDB `roadPacks` store. `js/roadgraph.js` then does on-device what OSRM does over HTTP:
snap, one Dijkstra per source costed in seconds, reconstruct paths. It returns the **same
`{D, T}` shape `osrmMatrix` returns**, which is why it drops in as the ladder's first rung
without a single downstream change.

The ladder is now **road graph → (planner OSRM / phone Google) → ORS → straight-line**. With a
district downloaded the billable rung is never reached; durations are real, so ETAs lose their
"(est.)" label; and the phone **produces** `legGeometryRoad` for the first time instead of only
decoding the planner's, so the route view draws actual roads offline.

Two consequences worth stating. The two-second hold no longer means "spend money" — a plain
tap is road-accurate wherever a pack covers the run, and the gesture only changes anything
outside it. And Optimize's flat `!navigator.onLine` refusal is gone; the gate now asks the real
question, which is whether the run can measure anything. Only geocoding a never-pinned address
still needs signal, and those park as they always have.

Four things here are load-bearing:

- **The repair pass is not optional.** A stop that snaps to no road, or an island with no
  road link, comes back `Infinity` — which would poison the solve and every metre total
  downstream. Those pairs fall back to crow-flies *individually*, and the run says so in its
  toast rather than patching silently.
- **`maps/*.pack` must never enter `sw.js`'s `SHELL`.** ⟳ Force update from GitHub re-fetches
  the whole shell; packs there would turn a routine app update into a many-megabyte download
  on boat signal.
- **A new routing provider has to be added to `js/planner-services.js`** — the name map *and*
  `createLastRunRecord`'s allow-list both whitelist provider strings, so an unlisted one is
  silently recorded as `haversine` forever.
- **The live-GPS-start crow-flies rewrite is now skipped on the local graph.** That rewrite
  exists to keep a fix out of a matrix somebody pays for; a downloaded district already routed
  it for free, so applying it there would swap a real road distance for an estimate.

The desktop planner is deliberately untouched: its OSRM has turn restrictions and turn
penalties this graph does not, so it stays the better answer where it's available. Distances
land within a few percent of it; durations drift a little more.

Packs are built by `tools/build-roadpack.mjs` — plain Node, no dependencies — from an `osmium`
export of the same Ontario `.pbf` already downloaded for OSRM, and committed under `maps/` so
GitHub Pages serves them like everything else. `tests/roadgraph.test.mjs` builds its fixtures
through that same writer, so a one-sided format change fails in the suite rather than in the
field. See [DEPLOY.md](../DEPLOY.md) §"Offline road maps for the phone".

## Fixed: the 🧭 Optimize button ate scroll attempts

`f3f5044` · `js/press-hold.js` (new), `js/worklist.js`, `css/capture.css`

A `touchstart` `preventDefault()` had been added to stop iOS selecting the button's label. It
does that — but it cancels *every* native default for the touch, panning included. Optimize
is a full-width 56px button, so it became a strip of the worklist the crew could not scroll
through: the only way past it was to start the swipe above or below the button.

Compounding it, `pointerup` had no movement check and pointer capture kept the pointer bound
to the button, so a drag that began there still counted as a short tap and started a route
optimization on an accidental brush.

The tap-vs-hold recognizer moved out to `js/press-hold.js` — pure, injectable timers, so the
decision is unit-tested rather than asserted as source text. It aborts the press past 10px of
travel and eats the click that follows a pointer gesture so no command runs twice. Selection
suppression is left to CSS, where it costs no scrolling.

**The landmine, recorded in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md):** never `preventDefault()` a
`touchstart` to stop text selection, and any press-and-hold must abort on movement.
