# 2026-07-30 — A finish clock that read as seven hours in the past, and one hold-up that wrecked the whole day's projection

One field report, with a screenshot taken at 11:36 in the morning:

> *"I think there's a bug with the route completion time and the number of meters I'll
> get to. It's saying I should be done at 11:28 and it's 11:35. I did 3 meters super
> early, then I was held up for a long time and didn't install the 4th until 10:30 — and
> my pace at 10:30 said I'd only get 3 done, which didn't make sense because I'd already
> done 3 and had the rest of the day."*

Three separate defects, all of them visible on that one card, all fixed here.

## The finish clock was formatted by a function that cannot express the evening

`js/compute/estimate.js`, `js/drive.js`, `tests/estimate.test.mjs`,
`tests/pace-live.test.mjs`

The card's own numbers pin its inputs exactly — `6 of 24 stops` and `~9 installs` mean
`done = 6`, `pendingCount = 18`, `willDo = 3` — and `routeFinishMin` is
`now + travel + stops × on-site`. Solve that for the printed `11:29` at a `now` of 11:36
and **there is no solution under 12:00**. The clock had not gone backwards. It had
wrapped: the real value was **1409 minutes — 23:29, twenty past eleven at night**.

`clockLabel` is a bare 12-hour readout with no meridiem and no day:

```js
const h = ((Math.floor(min / 60) + 11) % 12) + 1;
return h + ':' + String(min % 60).padStart(2, '0');
```

That is correct for what it was written for. The two **horizons** it labels — `3:45` and
`4:45 OT` — are compile-time constants that cannot leave the afternoon, so the missing
am/pm reads right by context. `routeFinishLabel` then reused it for a number that is
*derived* and has **no ceiling at all**, and every hour past noon came back wearing a
morning's clothes. 23:29 printed `11:29`; 24:15 printed `12:15`; 35:29 would have printed
`11:29` again.

`finishLabel` is the new sibling, and the split is the point: **`clockLabel` is only ever
safe for the fixed horizons; anything derived gets `finishLabel`.** It carries am/pm
always — `6:05 pm`, `1:10 am` — and appends a day marker (`1:10 am +1d`) past 24 hours,
where a meridiem on its own would be the same ambiguity in a new hat.

Worth recording that **the rest of the card was already honest**: the line was painted
amber, because `routeFinishMin > pace.horizonMin` fired correctly against the real 23:29.
Only the text lied, which is exactly why it was believed.

## A hold-up was charged as time spent installing, and it never washed out

`js/compute/cadence.js` (new), `js/worklist.js`, `tests/cadence.test.mjs`

`onsitePerStopReal` measured the day's on-site pace as the **mean** of its WO→WO gaps.
A day is a handful of gaps, and a morning that goes 06:45 · 07:10 · 07:35 · **10:30**
produces `[25, 25, 175]` — a mean of **75 minutes a stop** for a crew that is really
doing 25. Worse, this is not a wobble that settles: every later projection re-reads the
same day, so one wait poisons the gauge until midnight. That is the installer's second
complaint, precisely — at 10:30 the phone believed each remaining meter would take an
hour and a quarter.

It is the **median** now. One hold-up costs that day one gap instead of all of them,
there is no threshold to tune, and — the part that decided it — it works on a day where
the crew logged nothing, which is the normal case in a truck. The spine had already
reached the same conclusion from the other end: `installerOnSiteFit` trims outliers by
residual before fitting. This is that idea at the size a single day comes in.

The second half is smaller but was plainly wrong: the call passed `[]` where
`computeGapsLocal` takes the day's downtime, so a delay the crew **had** written down was
still counted as installing. `paceContext` already had `cached.downtime` and
`cached.eodTravel` open; they are threaded through and netted out of each gap now, the
same rows `capture.js` passes to the same gap model for the end-of-day review. Insurance
rather than the fix — a hold-up is usually only recorded at end of day, if at all — but
it cost nothing and removed a real inconsistency between two callers of one function.

The arithmetic moved into `js/compute/cadence.js` so it is a pure module the test suite
can drive against a real day, rather than a private function inside a DOM-bound file.

## An 18 km/h "driving speed" priced the rest of the route

`js/worklist.js`

`routeTravel` prices the remaining legs at `liveMetrics().avgMovingSpeed`, and the same
screenshot's own tiles reported **18 km/h** on a rural day where the concessions are
driven at 60–80.

`avgMovingSpeed` is distance ÷ (elapsed − idle), and "idle" is anything at or under
`IDLE_SPEED_MS` — 0.5 m/s, **1.8 km/h**. The drive recorder runs whenever the PWA is
open, *including while the crew is on foot at a meter*, and walking a property at 4 km/h
clears that bar comfortably. So dooryard and on-foot minutes land in the numerator as
"moving" and drag the average down without bound.

The guard was `speed > 1` m/s — 3.6 km/h — which catches a phone that never moved and
nothing else. 18 km/h sailed through and priced 18 remaining legs at **2¼ hours instead
of 45 minutes**: about 90 minutes stolen from the afternoon, three stops off the
projection, and 90 minutes added to the finish clock. There is now a
`MIN_BELIEVABLE_SPEED_MPS` floor of 25 km/h, under which the nominal
`ESTIMATE_SPEED_KMH` prices the route instead.

Deliberately **not** fixed by changing `avgMovingSpeed` itself. That number is also the
office-side leg summary uploaded to `DriveTracks`, read by the map viewer and
`installerOnSiteFromTracks`, so redefining it reaches well past this projection. The
floor is local to the route pricing and leaves every other consumer alone.

## Verification

The reported card was reproduced before anything was changed — first as a unit case
(`nowMin 696`, `pendingCount 18`, `remainingTravelMin 137`, `onsitePerStop 32` returns
every number on the screenshot, `routeFinishLabel` included), then end-to-end in a
headless browser with the morning seeded into `dayCache`, where the old build printed
`Route done ~12:15` for a route finishing at **00:15 the next morning**. The same harness
on the fixed build reads `Route done ~5:50 pm`, still amber. Both cases are now tests.
