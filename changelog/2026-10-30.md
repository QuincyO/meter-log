# 2026-10-30 — Turning the photo bot on starts Ollama

## The tray starts Ollama with the bot

The owner turned the work-order bot on from the tray, and the dot went green with the
bot polling Telegram. Ollama wasn't running, though, so every photo would have been
answered with "the AI reader (Ollama) didn't answer". Nothing on the tray showed that
the reader was missing.

**What shipped:** `tray.ps1`'s `Start-Bot` now calls `Start-Ollama` first. It does
nothing when the config's `ollamaUrl` points at another machine, or when an `ollama` or
`ollama app` process is already running. Otherwise it opens the Ollama desktop app from
its default install folder. If the desktop app isn't installed but `ollama` is on PATH,
it runs `ollama serve` hidden instead. When Ollama can't be found at all, the "On"
balloon says photos can't be read.

**The trap:** turning the bot off does **not** stop Ollama. Other tools on this PC use
the same Ollama, so the bot's tray leaves it running.

**Verification:** with the tray and every Ollama process stopped, opening the
**Work-order bot** shortcut brought Ollama up (`/api/version` answered 0.32.13) and the
bot logged `@MeterLogReaderBot is running`. The script parses under Windows PowerShell
5.1.

[tray](../tools/wo-bot/tray.ps1), [setup](../DEPLOY.md).

## Photo-bot orders go up lowest number first

The owner asked for orders from the photo bot to land on the worklist sorted by
work-order number, lowest first. Before, they arrived in the order the photos were
read (the handheld's screen order).

**What shipped:** `sendOrder` in `extract.mjs` sorts a copy of the batch, and
`handleSend` in `bot.mjs` posts that to `queueWorklistOrders`. Nothing changes on the
spine: `queueWorklistOrders` appends inbox rows in the order it's sent, and
`claimWorklistInbox` numbers their `order` in that same sequence.

**The trap:** only the upload is sorted. The batch and `/list` stay in reading order,
because the user checks them against the handheld's screen top to bottom, and a sorted
list would make a misread harder to spot.

**Verification:** a new test in `tests/wo-bot-extract.test.mjs` checks the sorted output
and that the batch itself isn't reordered. `node --test "tests/*.test.mjs"` passes (766).

[extraction](../tools/wo-bot/extract.mjs), [bot](../tools/wo-bot/bot.mjs),
[tests](../tests/wo-bot-extract.test.mjs), [user guide](../USER-GUIDE.md).

## Fill in addresses: chips and Save sit above the keyboard

The No. / unit box takes focus on every order of the **Fill in addresses**
walkthrough, so the phone keyboard opens straight away. It covered the recent-street
chips and **Save & next**, which were below the fields. Every order cost a scroll down
to tap a chip and another to save.

**What shipped:** the street chips and the ambiguous-town chips now sit between the
WO# and the address fields. **Save & next** moved into the sticky header, top right,
opposite ‹ Worklist. ‹ Back / Skip › stay below the fields. Only the markup moved. Every
id is unchanged, so `worklist-address-fill.js` needs no change, and a phone holding an
older module or older `index.html` still binds everything.

**The trap:** the header is tight on a narrow phone. At 360 px, "Save & finish" (the
last order's label) pushed "addresses" under the button. The title now wraps to two
lines, and a `max-width:374px` rule shrinks the title and the button text a little.

**Verification:** a new test in `tests/worklist-address-fill.test.mjs` checks that
Save is in the header and that the chips come before the fields.
`node --test "tests/*.test.mjs"` passes (767). Headless Edge screenshots at 390 and
360 px show both labels with no overflow.

[markup](../index.html), [styles](../css/capture.css),
[tests](../tests/worklist-address-fill.test.mjs), [user guide](../USER-GUIDE.md).
