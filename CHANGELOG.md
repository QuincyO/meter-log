# Changelog

What shipped, newest first — one line per day, each linking to that day's page in
[`changelog/`](changelog/). `AGENTS.md` and `ARCHITECTURE.md` describe how the app works
*now*; this records how it got that way, and the bug or the reasoning behind each change,
which a diff alone doesn't carry.

**Dates, not version numbers.** There is no release process: GitHub Pages serves the repo
root and CI deploys `Code.gs`, both from `main`, so a merge to `main` *is* the release.

**Adding an entry?** Write the day page and add its row here, in the same commit as the
change — see `AGENTS.md` §"Standing workflow rules". The automated
`Nightly sheet export — <date>` commits are the spine writing its own Sheet snapshot back to
the repo, and are not listed.

> In Obsidian: `Ctrl+O` and type a date to jump straight to a day, or open a row below.
> The vault root is the repo root, so every page here is a real note.

| [2026-07-31](changelog/2026-07-31.md) | **A 🔓/🔒 toggle: the day is settled when the installer says it is** — this was the fourth change in a week to "which orders are on today", so the mechanism that kept needing fixing was the *inferring*, not any of the inferences; unlocked, the meters/day target and the plan date are live and 🧭 Optimize re-plans every day at whatever number is in the box, and locked, day 1 is frozen exactly as it stands and nothing moves work in or out of it — not a logged stop, not a Download, not another Optimize — until it's unlocked by hand, with an added order raising the same *Add to today / Leave for later* question and slotting in without pushing anything off the end. `needsCommit`, `dayCapacity`, `opts.max`/`opts.fromTags`/`opts.replan`/`opts.resize` and the whole `freeReplan`/`midReplan`/`exhausted` triangle are deleted; the press is the only commit there is. The lock is **one date** (locked iff it equals the plan day), so a new day releases it with nothing to expire. **Unlocked is deliberately not "no `day1Count`"** — `applyTodayAnchor` also runs after every logged stop, on the Drive screen's automatic refresh, on Download and at boot, and re-chunking there would refill day 1 from the front of pending for anyone who simply hadn't pressed 🔒, which is yesterday's bug back on a timer; there are three answers, and only the meters/day box asks for the re-chunk. The variant switch and the drag write-back carry the count too, or the lock leaked through both. The office gets the same toggle on the planner, synced by one appended `WorklistPlans.dayLockDate` column whose blank is a **real value** (so the spine guards on key presence, not truthiness, or unlocking from the phone would never reach the sheet). **And the road-distance button stops answering with the whole week** — two orders 2 km apart with the tile reading 241 km, which was correct arithmetic over a four-day plan with one far-off order at the bottom of the list, summed into the figure a crew reads as today's driving; nothing was double-counting a commute (a day's first stop has always been charged 0 and the drive out lives in `homeLegMeters*`, which no total reads), the headline was simply unscoped. The phone's tiles and counts line price **day 1** now, and the counts line takes its metres from `liveDayMeters` — the same live `day` field the day dividers bucket on — so the headline and Day 1's divider are the same number *by construction* rather than two sums that happen to agree; the tiles bucket on each variant's own saved day, so road-vs-straight stays a like-for-like choice. The office keeps the whole plan and now says so (`241 km (road) · 4 days`), and the phone gains a caption carrying what its tiles leave out, because there is nothing to hover on a phone. Two comments that described the opposite of their code are fixed with it — including the day divider's own tooltip, which told the installer the drive out was in the number beside it. **And the Drive screen's automatic refresh moves 5 → 3 minutes and starts timing your arrival from where the truck actually is** — every remaining ETA hangs off "where does the next drive start?", and the answer had been the pin of the last order *completed*, which is right in a driveway and wrong mid-drive or after any walk-up that never matched a worklist order; the GPS recorder had been watching all along and simply never let the coordinates out of the module, so the origin is a ladder now (live fix → last completed pin → muster point) and a live fix moved a seeded first-stop ETA from 08:59 to 11:08 on position alone. The 2-minute freshness bound is the feature rather than a knob: a web app gets no GPS in the background, so a Maps hand-off freezes the newest fix at the driveway the crew pulled out of and it stays there looking perfectly good for the whole leg — past the window it decays back to the old origin. `routeTravel` re-prices the **first** leg from the same fix so the arrival time and the *Route done ~* clock aren't two models of one day, and the Drive card finally **shows** the number — `Arrive ~11:08 · in 129 min`, off the persisted `scheduledEta` rather than a second estimate, greyed and labelled `(stale)` when the last solve failed. A phone that never armed tracking reaches none of it and behaves exactly as before: no prompt, no GPS wakeup, nothing on screen. **And the Boat/Land switch comes off the capture page** — boat work has wound down, so a control that can only be set wrong is worse than no control; `js/work-mode.js` **forces** the value rather than defaulting to it, which is the whole change, since boat *was* the default and every phone in the field therefore has a literal `workMode='boat'` a new default would never consult. The drive recorder was the second reader of that key and cannot import `capture.js` (which imports *it*), so the lock lives in a module of its own — left alone it would have kept tagging every drive leg blank, which the spine reads as boat. The teams admin page keeps its switch on its own `teamsViewMode` key, because the office still has to reach historical boat teams and flipping the office view should never repaint a crew's phone. **And the Drive page's arrival times stop being measured in straight lines** — the phone already routes on a downloaded district, so 🧭 Optimize produced ETAs measured on real roads and then `applyTodayAnchor` (after *every* logged stop, on the 3-minute refresh, on Download, at boot) re-timed the whole day with haversine × 1.3 ÷ 50 km/h, which meant every re-anchor was a downgrade and the good numbers lasted until the first meter was logged; `estimateTravel` is a ladder now — the district's road graph, else the road metres the last solve saved, else crow-flies — and on eight fully-routable stops the day's last arrival moved **12:44 → 13:41**, +1 min at the first stop and +57 by the eighth, which is the shape of "fine in the morning, nonsense after lunch" and the optimistic direction that fails late rather than early. Coverage is judged on the **stops**, never on the truck, so a fix at the edge of the district can't veto a day the pack covers; `loadGraph` takes `{switchActive:false}` because it is not a pure read and a background re-anchor must not silently re-point the crew's map; and `wlTimesEstimated` now describes the **model** (`travel.measured`) rather than the caller, which retires two Download-time writes that were cleared on the line before an `applyTodayAnchor` that immediately re-derived them. `routeTravel` re-prices its first leg through the same graph, so the arrival time and the *Route done ~* clock are one model rather than two that agree on a good day. Between two Optimizes the pending set only ever shrinks, so the stop×stop matrix is cached and served by index remapping and only the truck's row is re-measured — 266 ms vs 12 ms on the committed pack, with `matrixRow` sharing `fillRow` with `matrix` so a warm cache can never change an ETA. **And the pace card stops telling a crew who are on track for twenty that they will finish thirteen** — at 12:31 with 13 done it projected *zero* of the eight stops still ahead over the next three and a quarter hours, directly above a card reading "Arrive 12:36 · in 5 min", while the route's own ETA chain landed the last of them at 14:45. Both of its inputs were wrong and in opposite directions, which is why it read as merely gloomy: remaining travel was 110.8 km ÷ the HUD's "average moving speed", which counts the crew standing at a property as driving and had sagged to 28 km/h, and on-site was the 16-minute median gap less a flat 10-minute drive over legs really driven in two, floored back up to 8. The 13th stop was an off-plan walk-up, which made it a controlled experiment: `done` rose while the route ahead did not change by a metre and the remaining drive still went 196 → 241 minutes, so the longer the crew spent installing the fewer installs they were promised. The 25 km/h floor added the day before did not fire because the number that beat it was 28, and raising it only moves the number that beats it — effective speed climbs with leg length, and this route ran 2.2 km a leg around Kearney in the morning and 13.9 with a 58.7 km haul in the afternoon. Both halves are priced by one `estimateTravel` lookup now, the same ladder the arrival badges use, over a frame carrying the done orders as well as the pending ones so the legs already behind the crew can be priced at all; each gap's own measured drive is netted out instead of a constant, so on-site falls out of the arithmetic rather than being guessed and then floored. `avgMovingSpeed` is left alone — the map viewer and `installerOnSiteFromTracks` read it — it just no longer prices this card. On the real day: `~13 · Route done 5:36 pm` → `~19 · 3:59 pm`, and the finish clock now lands on the last stop's own ETA instead of 74 minutes past it |
