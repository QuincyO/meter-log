Installer lifetime metrics

All-time rollups, rebuilt at end of day — the date range above does not apply. Installer chips do.

Lifetime by installer

Each installer's all-time record, rebuilt every night from their closed days. Avg log is the average minutes between two of their consecutive logs — lower is faster. Recent 30 is the same average over their last 30 workdays: ▼ means they've been faster than their lifetime pace lately, ▲ slower, no arrow means steady. UTI % is the share of all logs that ended as a UTI. Down min/day is their logged downtime averaged per working day.

Avg minutes per log — land vs boat

The same minutes-per-log pace, split by work mode. A land day and a boat day are different jobs — moving between boat stops takes longer — so comparing an installer to themselves across modes says more than one blended number. Shorter bar = faster. An installer who has only worked one mode shows a single bar.

Dwell model (feeds route ETAs)

The measured numbers the route planner uses to predict arrival times. On-site is the minutes an installer actually spends at a stop; +Per meter is the extra time when one stop has a second meter; Travel is their real driving pace in minutes per km. Source says how those were measured: GPS — from the phone's recorded driving legs (the most direct: the parked time between one drive ending and the next starting, counted only when a log landed inside it and close to where the truck parked — so a lunch or a supply run is never measured as an install); FIT — a line fitted through the installer's stop-to-stop gaps against distance, net of logged downtime (the flat part of the line is on-site time, the slope is travel speed); PACE — not measured yet: too few closed days, so the planner still falls back on the generic pace guess until enough data builds up.
